# Proposal (AIM 6.3) — Organisation, Project, Software, Service: what a thing means, at any level

Status: **agreed and written into the specification (AIM 6.3, released with 6.4), 2026-11-08** — drafted 2026-11-07, revised after a second model's review and a third review of Parts 4–5 · Author: Michael & Claude working session · Affects: §2 (`nature`, `kind`), §5 (traceability, sibling facet files), §6.0 (the facet list, promoted forms), §6.2 (Contract becomes Operation), §6.3 (Flow becomes Process; a Process before its steps), §6.4 (Persona: unchanged in meaning, one sentence), §6.8 (Capability is a function), §3 and §5 (a requirement's source), new §6.12–§6.15 (Organisation, Project, Software, Service), §7 (endpoints), §8 (provider), §9 (binding sidecar spellings), §10 (diagnostics), §11–§13 (promotion wording)

## The principle

**A facet expresses what something means, consistently at any level of the tree. Nesting expresses how those things fit together in the model** — where a thing is described and how widely it is shared (§4: tree position determines ownership; §11: a thing lives at its consumers' lowest common ancestor, and the depth of its address says how widely it is shared). Nesting says nothing else: not that one organisation is a member of another, not whether a software is built on its own. A department is an Organisation whether it is the root of a workspace or three levels down; the tree says whose model describes it, and the facet says what it is.

## Where this comes from

Amy's Studio, the two days 2026-11-06 and 2026-11-07, with tester feedback from the days before, and two reviews of the drafts by a second model.

- An owner wrote "we have a shipping department" and Amy built a process. A department is not work; it is a part of the organisation with work of its own. The product had typed the workspace root `company` and had just added `area` for a department under it: two names for one thing, told apart by depth. Looking for the second name showed that the language has no form for an organisation at all before it is an intent — nor for a project, nor for a software module — while a person has one (Persona) and a process has one (Flow).
- The owner's test for an intent of its own: *does this thing have its own intent to do something, a motivation?* Yes → it qualifies. A person, an organisation, a project, a software, a system the work uses, and a process answer yes. A record, a body of knowledge, an event, a view answer no.
- The owner's rule for every facet: *a facet until it earns an intent*, by that test, decided by the modelling agent's judgement, never a doctrine — "if Amy in one case decides a Persona to be a facet and with the same input and a different model it's an intent, nothing goes bad; it's structure and can be adjusted."
- Modelling a music delivery system, the owner named a *DDEX 4.x generator* as a capability — something Amy could even code herself if required — and *SAP* as a system "I can connect with an API/MCP". §6.8 lets both be a Capability. They are two things: a function the work must be able to do, and a system the work consumes.
- Two drafts grouped the new facets with Persona, first under an invisible family name "Entity", then under the word "actor". The first review found Entity too broad: Persona keeps its clear human meaning, a person or a human role, and an organisation is a legal or collective person, not a natural one. Persona and Organisation share a participant meaning — both perform work — without being one facet.
- The second review corrected the next draft on five points, all applied here: a Project is not a performer; nesting implies neither membership nor deployment; Software and Service are told apart by their role in the model, not by ownership; build decisions stay explicit, never derived from `nature` or placement; and "Decided" claimed more than was agreed.
- The same rule, applied to work: the facet is `## Flow:`, the intent is `nature: flow`, and every owner, the product, BPMN and this specification's own prose call the thing a process. The owner (2026-11-08): rename it.
- And to its unit: the facet is `## Contract:`, every rule of the specification says "the operation", a Capability lists its Operations and a mapping has an Operation Map, and to an owner a contract is what their company signs. The owner (2026-11-08): rename it; a dry run on the NASA example renamed 36 headings and 99 addresses with every reference still resolving.
- Writing the NASA example put `[satisfies]` on eleven requirement bullets — a child's rule pointing at the ancestor's rule it comes from — which §7 forbids (a requirement item is never a source). The model wanted to say *where a rule comes from* and had no way. The same gap stood queued since 6.2 as "a statement's source", and ArchiMate's motivation layer calls it a driver.
- Two auditors reviewed the product proposal (`aim/work/2026-11-07-intent-kinds-proposal.md` in the Amy repository). Their points that touch the language are folded in: one explicit kind per intent that content never re-derives; a consumer's Capability and a provider's model stay two things; promotion needs a transform that carries everything.

## Part 1 — four new facets

### The problem

§6.4 defines Persona as "an actor: an entity that exists (an employee, a client) or a role". Three things do not fit it and have no facet of their own:

- an organisation that acts in the work — a department that packs, a supplier that delivers, a partner club — which is not a human and will not be written as one;
- a project — a goal and an end — before it has processes of its own;
- a software, or a part of one, before it has records, operations and views of its own.

Each of them is an intent once it has earned a level (§11), and the product needs a word for that intent's kind. But before that, a first mention has nowhere to land except a Requirements bullet or a premature intent, and a premature intent is exactly the "wrapper child with exactly one facet" of §10. A person has the Persona facet for this stage and the promoted persona after it; a piece of work has the Flow facet and the promoted process. The others need the same path, each as what it is.

### Proposal

**Persona is unchanged.** Its name, its block, its rules. One sentence in its Purpose makes the boundary explicit: "A Persona is a human: a person who exists (an employee, a client) or a role people hold (the approver of a claim). A group that acts as one is an Organisation (§6.12); a system that acts is a Software (§6.14) or a Service (§6.15)."

**Four facets are added**, each with its own block. Where one performs work, the performer rules of §6.4 apply to it by reference — a rule shared is not a family.

```markdown
## Organisation: <Name>
## Project: <Name>
## Software: <Name>
## Service: <Name>
```

**§6.12 Organisation.**

> **Purpose.** An Organisation is a group that works together and acts as one: a company, a club, a department, a team, a site, a supplier, a partner. It is a legal or collective person, never a natural one; the people in it are Personas. It declares what the group is, what it reaches and what work it performs. It is the form of such a group before it has earned an intent of its own, and the same facet wherever it is described.
>
> **Form.**
>
> | Sub-block | Required | Holds |
> |---|---|---|
> | `### Label` | no | The name as people say it (§3). |
> | `### Summary` | yes | What the group is and does. |
> | `### Role` | no | One bullet describing it as an actor; may carry `[plays](aim:<address>)` to a shared Organisation. |
> | `### Access` | no | Bullets with the Views or intents it `accesses` and the operations it `invokes`, as §6.4 writes them. |
>
> **Example.**
>
> ```markdown
> ## Organisation: Shipping
>
> ### Label
>
> Versand
>
> ### Summary
>
> Gets every order out of the house.
>
> ### Access
>
> - Packs and hands over — [invokes](aim:root.orders#Operation:PackOrder)
> ```
>
> **Edges.** Declares `accesses`, `invokes` and `plays` (to an Organisation). Receives `plays` from local organisation roles filled by it; Authz entries name it without an edge.
>
> **Rules.** When it performs work, §6.4's performer rules apply (who performs, permission and performance distinct, owed work). **Facet until earned:** it stays a facet on the level that shares it (§11) until it has a purpose, requirements, entry points or several facets of its own — the Architect's test: does it have its own intent to do something? Either reading leaves a valid model; a later promotion or merge (§12) adjusts it; this is a judgement, not a diagnostic. **Promoted Organisation:** an intent with `nature: organisation`, at the level that held it (§11, §12); it holds Children (its processes, its Personas, its Records, its Services) and keeps top-level `## Role` and `## Access` only while it still acts in other work. The root of a project may be an Organisation. Where an Organisation is described says whose model describes it, not whose member it is: a department and a supplier may both be described under the same level.
>
> **Diagnostics.** Hard error: no Summary; an intent used as an actor without a top-level `## Role` (§6.4). Informational: an Organisation that performs, reaches, plays and is permitted nothing.
>
> **Since.** 6.3.

**§6.13 Project.**

> **Purpose.** A Project is a goal with an end: the website relaunch, done by March. It contains work; it does not perform it — the people are Personas, a project team is an Organisation. It is the form of such a goal before it has earned an intent, and it recurs anywhere in the tree with logic of its own. What the organisation *keeps* as projects (an agency's client projects, rows with a client and a deadline) is a Record, not a Project.
>
> **Form.**
>
> | Sub-block | Required | Holds |
> |---|---|---|
> | `### Label` | no | The name as people say it (§3). |
> | `### Summary` | yes | The goal and its end, in words. |
>
> **Example.**
>
> ```markdown
> ## Project: Relaunch
>
> ### Summary
>
> The new website replaces the old one by the end of March.
> ```
>
> **Edges.** Declares none; receives none. It is never a performer, never named in Authz, never a `plays` target.
>
> **Rules.** Facet until earned, as Organisation. **Promoted Project:** `nature: project`, at the level that held it, holding its processes, people, software and sub-projects as Children; the work in it is performed by the Personas and Organisations it holds or names. The root of a project may be a Project. No construct for the end date: the end is words in the Summary; a deadline the work must keep is a Trigger (§6.7).
>
> **Diagnostics.** Hard error: no Summary; a Project with `### Role` or `### Access`, named in Authz, or the source or target of an edge. **Since.** 6.3.

**§6.14 Software.**

> **Purpose.** A Software is software whose structure and behaviour are described here: a website, an app, a system, or a part of one, with its Records, Operations, Views and Processes beneath it, at the root of a workspace or nested inside another intent. It acts when it performs a step on its own ("the webshop sends the confirmation"). Who builds it, who owns it and where it runs are not what makes it a Software; a system that is only consumed through a connection is a Service (§6.15), and the same software may be a Software where it is described and a Service wherever it is consumed. A function the work must be able to do is a Capability (§6.8).
>
> **Form.**
>
> | Sub-block | Required | Holds |
> |---|---|---|
> | `### Label` | no | The name as people say it (§3). |
> | `### Summary` | yes | What it is and does. |
> | `### Role` | no | One bullet describing it as an actor. |
> | `### Access` | no | The operations it `invokes` when it performs a step on its own. |
>
> **Example.**
>
> ```markdown
> ## Software: Billing
>
> ### Summary
>
> Issues and sends the invoices of the shop.
>
> ### Access
>
> - Sends the invoice — [invokes](aim:root.webshop#Operation:SendInvoice)
> ```
>
> **Edges.** Declares `invokes` when it acts; receives none. **Rules.** §6.4's performer rules when it acts — a system as the doer of a step; facet until earned, as Organisation. **Promoted Software:** `nature: software`, at the level that held it, with its Records, Operations, Views, Processes and Bindings (§9) of its own. A Software described inside another Software is a part of that description, nothing more. **Build decisions are explicit.** Whether a Software is built, deployed or listed on its own is a fact of its realization — a build target, a Binding (§9) — stated there; it is never derived from `nature`, from its parent or from its depth. The root of a project may be a Software.
>
> **Diagnostics.** Hard error: no Summary. **Since.** 6.3.

**§6.15 Service.**

> **Purpose.** A Service is a system consumed through a defined connection — an ERP, a CRM, a carrier's portal, a code host, or a software of the organisation's own that another part of it reaches only through its interface — named by the system (SAP, Zoho, GitHub). It acts when it performs a step ("SAP posts the invoice"). It is the consumer's naming of the system; the system's own model is the provider intent of §8, outside the consuming tree, which may itself be a Software described elsewhere. A function the work must be able to do, which a Service may answer, is a Capability (§6.8).
>
> **Form.** Label (no), Summary (yes: what the organisation uses it for), Role and Access (no), `### Bindings` (no: the connection that answers it, §8, §9).
>
> **Example.**
>
> ```markdown
> ## Service: SAP
>
> ### Summary
>
> Our ERP: orders, invoices and stock.
>
> ### Access
>
> - Posts the invoice — [invokes](aim:root.billing#Operation:PostInvoice)
> ```
>
> **Edges.** Declares `invokes` when it acts; receives none. A Service additionally answers Capabilities through a mapping (§8); that is not an edge.
>
> **Rules.** §6.4's performer rules when it acts. **A Service is connected, not promoted.** It earns its intent by being connected: the provider's model, read from the connection or shipped prepared, `nature: service`, read-only, outside the consuming tree (§8). The facet stays as the consumer's naming of it, connected through its own `### Bindings`; a mapping's `### Target` names the provider intent, as §8 has it (decided 2026-11-08, below). One Service may answer several Capabilities on several levels.
>
> **Diagnostics.** Hard error: no Summary. Informational: a Service whose Bindings bind nothing (not connected). **Since.** 6.3.

**The kind of an intent (§2 `nature`).** `nature` gains `organisation`, `project`, `software`, `service` beside `persona` and the others. One intent carries one `nature`. It stays what §2 says it is — a projection hint that MUST NOT affect resolution, edge legality or synthesis — and it is explicit: a tool MUST NOT change it from content (a Schema added to a persona does not make it a record), and a tool MAY refuse to open an intent whose nature it does not know how to present. A root intent may be of any kind: a workspace is its root intent, and the root is an organisation, a project, a software, a process or a persona. The kind of a child is independent of its parent's. Nothing operational follows from `nature`: not what may be nested under what, not what is built.

**§6.0 facet list.** "AIM 6.2 has eleven facets: …" becomes "AIM 6.3 has fifteen facets: Record, Operation, Process, Persona, View, Event, Trigger, Capability, Style, State, Knowledge, Organisation, Project, Software and Service". The promoted-forms sentence reads "Record, Persona, Process, Organisation, Project and Software have defined promoted forms; a Service is connected (§6.15)".

**§5 traceability.** "Behavioral entry points are a Persona accessing a View/screen intent" reads "a Persona or an Organisation accessing …". The chain is written with the names of Parts 4 and 5: "a Trigger starting a Process or an Operation. Views expose Operations; Operations may invoke Processes; Operations and Processes read or mutate Records, consult Knowledge and emit Events". "Knowledge joins it only as what operations consult" reads "Knowledge joins it only through `consults`, from an Operation, a Process, a View or an intent with Steps (§7)" — the sentence was narrower than its row.

**§7 endpoints.** The rows list the facets explicitly, no group word: `invokes` from process, view, operation, persona, organisation, software, service, intent; `accesses` from persona, organisation, intent with Role; `plays` from persona to persona or intent with Role, and from organisation to organisation; `triggered-by` to process / operation / view / trigger / persona / organisation / software / service / intent (Process and Operation are the names of Parts 4 and 5; a row reads the same under the old spelling). "From a Persona or an intent with a Role, performs it directly" reads "from a Persona, an Organisation, a Software, a Service or an intent with a Role, performs it directly". A Project is in no row. Intents as endpoints are unchanged: an intent with a top-level `## Role` is an actor whatever its nature.

**§11, §12, §13.** "Promote substantive Persona/Record entities" reads "Persona, Organisation, Project, Software or Record entities"; §13's exemption for "Personas as roles" extends to Organisations as roles. The promote transform (§12) carries, with the addresses, everything the realization attached to the facet: the rows of a Record promoted with it, the people who hold a Persona, the permissions its Authz entries gave, the connection a Service is bound to. A transform that drops one of these is not a valid transform.

### Diagnostics summary

- Hard: an Organisation, Project, Software or Service without a Summary; a Project with Role or Access, in Authz, or on an edge; two facets of one type and name in one intent, as today.
- Informational: a Service not connected; the §6.4 performer conditions applied to any facet that acts; `nature` contradicting content, as today.

### Existing models stay valid

Every 6.2 file is valid 6.3 source, unchanged. Among the facets of this part nothing is renamed or deprecated; four headings and four `nature` values are added. No edge, no node and no resolution changes meaning. (The renames of this proposal are Parts 4 and 5.)

## Part 2 — Capability: the function, apart from what provides it

### The problem

§6.8 says a Capability is "an outside system or service the work calls through operations … named by what it offers, or by the system the owner chose when that choice is the owner's decision (`SAP`, `GitHub`)". So `Capability: SAP` and `Capability: DDEXGenerator` are the same kind of thing in the language. They are not: SAP is a system one connects to through an API or MCP; generating DDEX 4.x is something the work must be able to do, which SAP, a library, a built-in service, or code written for the purpose might answer. §8 already keeps the provider's model apart from the consumer's Capability and joins them through a mapping; the one word for both sides is what muddles a conversation ("the company's *Stream* and the connected *Stream API* were two unrelated things", Amy, 2026-11-01).

### Proposal

- **A Capability is a function the work must be able to do**, operating on knowledge, resources, data: `## Capability: DDEXGenerator`, named by what it does — generate DDEX, convert currency, render a PDF, send e-mail — never by a vendor. It is the base classifier beneath Software, Service and the tool's own services: what is needed, apart from what provides it. §6.8's Purpose loses "or by the system the owner chose when that choice is the owner's decision (`SAP`, `GitHub`)"; such a name is a Service. Its Form, Edges and coverage rules are unchanged: it declares the operations the work relies on, is required by alias, and is resolved operation by operation.
- **A Capability is answered, never promoted.** It is answered by a Service's operation through the §8 mapping, by a Software described in the workspace (its Operation bound as the Capability's realization), by a direct Binding (a library, a built-in service of the tool), or by nothing yet. A Capability no Service, Software, Binding or mapping answers is what a performed intent needs supplied (§6.8, unchanged). *Noted, not proposed here:* a tool may offer to answer a Capability with code it builds when the work requires it; in the language that is a Binding like any other.
- **Diagnostics.** Informational: a Capability whose name is a vendor or product name the workspace also names as a Service (reads as the Service). Migration does not rewrite it (decided 2026-11-08, below).

### Existing models stay valid

A `Capability: SAP` of 6.2 remains a valid Capability. It is flagged informationally, never rejected.

## Part 3 — a Process before its steps (no change, stated)

§6.3 already makes `### Steps` *expected*, not required. The proposal adds one sentence to §6.3's rules so that the first form of a process is named: "A Process with a Summary and no Steps is a process that is named but not yet worked out. It is addressable, may be invoked from a step (a sub-process handed over by name), and is promoted like any Process once it has steps, operations, records or requirements of its own. Its missing steps are the informational condition of a sequence with no entry point, never a hard error."

## Part 4 — Flow becomes Process

### The problem

The facet is `## Flow:`, the intent it becomes is `nature: flow`, and the thing is called a process by every owner, by the product, by BPMN, and by this specification's own prose — "promoted process", "a Flow or promoted process", "a process with its own operations". Two words for one thing, which is what this proposal removes everywhere else: a thing has one word before and after it earns its intent. Flow is the diagrammer's word from 3.1, when AIM described software and a sequence of operations inside an app; the thing itself — one piece of work done in order, with its steps, decisions, waits and error path — is a process wherever it lives.

### Proposal

- `## Process: <Name>` replaces `## Flow: <Name>`; `nature: process` replaces `nature: flow`. §6.3 is titled Process and its Purpose reads "A Process is one piece of work done in order: its steps, where it branches, and what happens on error"; "flow-natured child" reads "process-natured child"; the §7 endpoint rows say `process` where they said `flow`; the §2 `nature` table lists `process`, and §2's inference reads "top-level Steps reads as process".
- **Every spelling of the type follows.** The type is serialized in more places than the heading, and the rename covers each: the heading `## Process:`; the frontmatter `nature: process` of a promoted process; the sibling facet file, `kind: process` in `<intent>.process.aim` (§2, §5 — the kind and the file name change together, since they MUST agree); the type in every address — an edge token `aim:<intent>#Process:<Name>`, a bracketed reference (a Trigger's anchor, 6.4's `Ends on`), a backticked address, a deprecated short form `#Process:<Name>`; and the deprecated binding sidecar's `## Bind: Process:<Name>` (§9).
- **The old spellings stay readable, and reading never writes.** `## Flow:`, `nature: flow`, `kind: flow` with `<intent>.flow.aim`, `#Flow:<Name>` in any address and `## Bind: Flow:<Name>` are deprecated spellings: tools MUST read each as its Process form, authors MUST NOT write them, and a conforming reader needs no write to do so. A connected provider intent (§8), which only its own source changes, is read under the old spellings as it is and is never rewritten. Migration MAY rewrite the deprecated spellings when it opens a model (§12), under the preservation rules of §3, as one change that renames the headings, the kinds and the file names and repoints every reference, shown to the owner like any change; a tool that rewrites rewrites them together, never the heading alone. `<intent>.flow.aim` and `<intent>.process.aim` are one file identity, as `kind: schema` and `kind: record` are.
- Operation is unchanged: one operation, what a step invokes, never a process.
- The chain then reads as the others do: a Process facet with a Summary names the work; it earns its intent, `nature: process`, with top-level `## Steps`.

### Diagnostics

Informational: `## Flow:`, `nature: flow`, `kind: flow` or `## Bind: Flow:` awaiting migration — never raised on a provider intent, which is read as it is. Hard error: a `Flow:` and a `Process:` of one name in one intent (one duplicate, as `Persona:` and `Person:` would have been); a `kind: flow` and a `kind: process` file for one intent (one sidecar).

### Existing models stay valid

Every 6.2 file is read as it is: `Flow` is `Process` under the old spelling — in the heading, the nature, the kind, the file name, the address and the sidecar alike. Reading changes nothing. A rewrite, where a tool offers one, changes no node, no edge and no resolution; it is a rename-class rewrite that repoints every inbound reference in one change.

## Part 5 — Contract becomes Operation

### The problem

The facet is `## Contract:`; §6.2 opens "A Contract is one operation"; every rule about it — performer, ownership, steps, Knowledge — says "the operation"; a Capability lists its `### Operations`; a mapping has an `### Operation Map` whose lines name the provider's Contracts without the word. The heading is the only place the word survives, and for the reader the language now puts in front of the block, a contract is the thing their company signs — a Vertrag. The name came from design by contract (Meyer, Eiffel): Expects as precondition, Ensures as postcondition, a contract between caller and implementation. That discipline is the block's sub-blocks, not its heading, and in software the word has since drifted to mean a whole interface (contract-first APIs, contract tests, smart contracts). An automation reads the unit it automates as an operation: OpenAPI's `operationId`, an MCP tool, an action.

### Proposal

- `## Operation: <Name>` replaces `## Contract: <Name>`. §6.2 is titled Operation; its Purpose reads "An Operation is one unit of work: what it needs, who may perform it, what it guarantees, and what it decides"; the §7 endpoint rows say `operation` where they said `contract` (`invokes` to an operation; `exposes` to an operation); `### Operation Map` and `### Operations` keep their names, which were always this word.
- **Every spelling of the type follows**, as in Part 4: the heading `## Operation:`; the sibling facet file, `kind: operation` in `<intent>.operation.aim` (§2, §5 — kind and file name together); the type in every address — an edge token `aim:<intent>#Operation:<Name>`, a bracketed reference (a State's moves and `Only in`), a backticked address (a State's guard, a mapping's Target), a deprecated short form, and a Decides outcome, `<intent>#Operation:<Name> → Decides[<Label>]`; and the deprecated binding sidecar's `## Bind: Operation:<Name>` (§9). An Operation has no `nature`; `### Operation Map` lines name operations without a type and are untouched.
- **The generic word is work.** Where the specification says "operation" for "an Operation or a Process" — "operations consult Knowledge", "the operation's Authz" — it says *work*: an Operation is one unit of work, a Process is work done in order, and a rule for both says work. "Owed work" and "performs work" already read so. *Work* is a word of the prose and never an endpoint: §7's rows name who may declare each edge, facet by facet, and stay the authority — a View consults Knowledge and satisfies a Requirement, a Trigger satisfies one, an intent with Steps consults, exactly as the rows list them — and no sentence that says *work* narrows a row. Where today's prose is narrower than its row, the row wins and the sentence is corrected to it (§5's Knowledge sentence, above).
- **The old spellings stay readable, and reading never writes.** `## Contract:`, `kind: contract` with `<intent>.contract.aim`, `#Contract:<Name>` in any address and `## Bind: Contract:<Name>` are deprecated spellings: tools MUST read each as its Operation form, authors MUST NOT write them, and a conforming reader needs no write to do so. A connected provider intent (§8) is read as it is and is never rewritten; the consumer reaches its operations through the mapping's `### Operation Map`, by name, under either spelling. Migration MAY rewrite the deprecated spellings when it opens a model (§12), in the one change with Part 4's — headings, kinds, file names and every reference together, shown to the owner. `<intent>.contract.aim` and `<intent>.operation.aim` are one file identity.
- The word *contract* stays an ordinary word: the specification may say "an operation's contract" for its Expects and Ensures together, and a Record of real contracts is `## Record: Contract` — which today collides with a facet type of the same name in one line.
- A Requirement stays what it is: what the intent commits to, joined to the work by `satisfies`.

### Diagnostics

Informational: `## Contract:`, `kind: contract` or `## Bind: Contract:` awaiting migration — never raised on a provider intent. Hard error: a `Contract:` and an `Operation:` of one name in one intent (one duplicate); a `kind: contract` and a `kind: operation` file for one intent (one sidecar).

### Existing models stay valid

Every 6.2 file is read as it is: `Contract` is `Operation` under the old spelling — in the heading, the kind, the file name, the address and the sidecar alike. Reading changes nothing; a rewrite, where a tool offers one, changes no node, no edge and no resolution.

## Part 6 — a requirement's source: `from`

### The problem

A requirement is a commitment with no memory of why. "Every order ships within two days" came from customers complaining; "wood needs an origin certificate" came from the EUDR; a process's rule came from a company rule one level up. When the complaint stops, the regulation changes or the company rule is rewritten, nothing in the model says which requirements are now in question, and two rules that collide cannot be weighed without knowing where each came from. The NASA example tried to write the third case with a `satisfies` edge on the bullet and produced eleven illegal triples. Owners say the reason in the same sentence as the rule; Amy keeps the rule and drops the reason, or asks again later.

### Proposal

A Requirements bullet MAY end with a source trailer:

```markdown
- **NET14** — The invoice is payable within fourteen days — from [company#Knowledge:AGB]
- **RANGE** — The count does not leave the hold without the range's clearance — from [nasa#Requirements[RANGE]]
- **TWO-DAYS** — Every order ships within two days — from: customers complained about delivery, 2026-10
```

- **Address or words.** `from [<address>]` names what the rule comes from: a Knowledge facet (a regulation, a document), a Requirements item of this or an ancestor intent (a rule derived from a rule), or an Event (a rule made because something happened). `from: <words>` keeps a reason that is not a thing in the model — an answer, a complaint, a decision — as prose. One trailer per bullet; several sources are written as one trailer with addresses separated by commas.
- **Not an edge.** The trailer is a structured reference like a State's moves or a Trigger's anchor: it derives `from` and its inverse `source-of` as structure, outside the closed taxonomy of §7, and outside traceability — a requirement with no source is complete.
- **What it is for.** A tool MAY list every requirement drawn from a source; when a Knowledge facet is re-read, a Requirements item is rewritten at its label, or an Event's emitter changes, the requirements drawn from it are flagged for a look (*stale source*, informational), as stale satisfier flags work whose requirement changed. Two requirements in conflict are shown with their sources.
- **Provenance at the grain of a bullet.** `from: the owner, <date>` and `from: proposed` tell apart what the owner said from what the authoring tool filled in; the file's provenance stays what it is.

### Diagnostics

Hard error: a `from [...]` address that does not resolve, or resolves to anything other than a Knowledge facet, a Requirements item or an Event; an edge token on a Requirements bullet (as today: a requirement item is a target of `satisfies`, never a source). Informational: *stale source*, as above.

### Existing models stay valid

Unchanged: a bullet without a trailer means what it meant; the trailer is read only where it is written.

## Part 7 — the facets, all together

| Facet | What it means | Performs work? | Intent (`nature`) | How it gets there |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Persona | a human: a person or a role people hold | yes | `persona` | promoted, at its level (unchanged) |
| Organisation | a group that works together: company, club, department, team | yes | `organisation` | promoted, at its level; the root may be one |
| Project | a goal with an end; it contains work | no | `project` | promoted; the root may be one |
| Software | software whose structure and behaviour are described here | yes, when it acts on its own | `software` | promoted; the root may be one |
| Service | a system consumed through a defined connection | yes, when it acts | `service` | connected: the provider's model, outside the tree (§8) |
| Process (was Flow) | a piece of work; a Summary alone names it | — | `process` | promoted (§6.3); the root may be one |
| Capability | a function the work must be able to do | — | — | answered (§8), never promoted |
| Record | a thing that is kept | — | `record` only with a Role or operations of its own (§6.1, unchanged) | promoted with its Persona when they are one thing |
| Operation (was Contract) | one unit of work: what it needs, who may perform it, what it guarantees, what it decides | — | — | — |
| View, Event, Trigger, Style, State, Knowledge | unchanged | — | — | — |

Fifteen facets: the eleven of 6.2, two of them renamed, plus four.

## Agreed (owner, 2026-11-07)

- The Entity grouping was too broad and is gone. Persona keeps its clear human meaning: a person or a human role. Persona and Organisation share a participant meaning — both perform work — without being one facet.
- Organisation, Project, Software and Service are facets of their own, each with its own block; the same facet at any level of the tree. A Project contains work and never performs it.
- Software is software whose structure and behaviour are described here; a Service is a system consumed through a defined connection; the same software may be both, in different places. A Capability is the function beneath either, named by what it does.
- Nesting is where a thing is described and how widely it is shared, nothing more. Build and deployment are explicit facts of the realization.
- What may hold what is guidance for the authoring tool, never a rule the language or an engine enforces.
- Facet until earned, for every facet, by the Architect's judgement; the cost of either reading is low because the transforms exist.
- A first-mentioned process lands as a Process facet with its Summary and no steps.
- Flow is renamed Process (owner, 2026-11-08): one word for the work before and after it earns its intent; Flow stays readable as the old spelling.
- A Requirements bullet may carry its source, `from [address]` or `from: words` (2026-11-08; applied in the NASA example, confirmed by the owner the same day): the reason behind a rule, kept at the grain where owners say it; not a facet, not an edge.
- Contract is renamed Operation (owner, 2026-11-08): the specification's own word for it, the automation's word for the unit it automates, and no longer the thing a company signs; Contract stays readable as the old spelling; *work* is the generic word for an Operation or a Process.
- The names: Project, not Initiative — true, but everyone understands Project. Service, not System — SAP, a carrier's portal and a small web API are all reached the same way, through a connection, and that is what the facet means; the spec keeps "system" as its generic word.

## Decided while building it into Amy (owner, 2026-11-08)

- A vendor-named `Capability:` is flagged, never rewritten (Part 2): turning it into a Service plus the functions its operations imply needs naming, which is the Architect's judgement in a conversation, not a mechanical step.
- A Service is connected through its own `### Bindings` only (Part 1, §6.15); a mapping's `### Target` stays a provider intent, as §8 has it. The alternative — a mapping may target the Service facet — was declined for now (the NASA README's findings 1–2).
