# Proposal (AIM 5.9) — Who does the work

Status: **draft for review** · Author: Michael & Claude working session, 2026-09-27 · Affects: §7.3, §7.4, §12.2, §1.2, §18, §13 · Syntax: **none**

## The problem

"Who does this step?" is the first question anyone asks of a process, and the answer is a commitment in
the strictest §1.4 sense: it is what a Reviewer holds a performance accountable to. The language answers
it only in part.

What 5.8 states:

- A Persona that `invokes` an operation performs it directly (§8.2, the `invokes` row; §8.6 records that
  `performs` was refused because it *is* `invokes` from a persona).
- A step whose only edges are `mutates`/`emits`/`reads` is work the flow performs itself (§7.3).
- A step with no edges is a human step (§7.3). The spec never says whose.

What 5.8 leaves open:

- **Who performs a human step.**
- **Whether work a Flow step calls inherits the Flow's performer**, and what stops the inheritance.
- **What `### Authz` means for performance.** §7.2's example reads as permission ("InternalReviewer
  only"). §18's own guidance puts "CFO approves…" in Authz, which reads as assignment.
- **Whether a Trigger that starts work, or a Persona that can reach the View exposing it, says anything
  about who does it.**

The 5.4 proposal recorded the first gap and left it open: *"A prose step has no performer — the reference
implementation infers it from neighbouring steps. For a process model, who performs a manual step is a
commitment, not an inference."*

### Worked failure: one model, two answers, one codebase

The reference implementation now has two readers of the same process model — the **guided runner**,
which runs cases for people, and the **process check**, which reads a Business Process as a map and
asks its owner questions. They answer "who does it?" differently:

| | Guided runner | Process check |
|---|---|---|
| `### Authz` names a role | assigns the work to that role, first | permission only, never the performer |
| Step opens with a Persona ("The Manager reviews…") | ignored | the step's performer |
| Work a Flow step calls | the work's Authz roles, else its invokers, else the Flow's roles | its own invoker, else the step's opening Persona, else the Flow's performer |
| A possessive ("the client's project") | names the Client | names no one |

(Reference implementation, `server/src/process-engine.js`: `rolesOf` — *"A guided case assigns work by
Authz first, then by the Personas that invoke it"*; `declaredPerformers` — *"`permitted` — Authz names who
MAY do it, never who does."*)

Take a model where the Persona `Clerk` invokes `ApproveInvoice` and its Authz says "Controller only". The
guided runner gives the step to the Controller. The process check asks: *"The model has 'Clerk' do
'ApproveInvoice', but says only 'Controller' may. Who does it?"* Both are conforming readings of 5.8,
because 5.8 does not say. This is the 5.5 proposal's warning, realized inside one tool: *"A performance
re-resolves ambiguity at every execution — two conforming performers of the same model may run
materially different processes."* A developer agent handed the exported bundle is a third reader, with
no rule at all.

### Worked example: why the order matters

The Expense Approval template:

```markdown
## Persona: Employee
### Access
- [invokes](aim:#Flow:ClaimExpense)

## Persona: Manager
### Access
- [invokes](aim:expenses.review#Contract:Review)

## Flow: ClaimExpense
### Steps
1. Employee submits the claim — [invokes](aim:expenses.submission#Contract:Submit).
2. Manager reviews the evidence — [invokes](aim:expenses.review#Contract:Review).
```

The Employee performs the Flow. Read by inheritance alone, step 2's `Review` is performed by the Employee
— the one person Review's own Expects forbids ("The reviewer is not the person who submitted the
claim"). It is the Manager's because Manager `invokes` Review directly, and because the step opens with
the Manager. A rule with inheritance and no stop, or a stop and no step subject, gets this model wrong.

## Scope sort (§1.4)

Who performs a piece of work is a commitment: a Reviewer can hold a performance to it ("step 2 was
performed by the Employee; the model says the Manager"), and swimlanes, handoffs and §18's
organizational drift ("a manual step with no accountable performer") all rest on it. How the performer
is notified, queued or authenticated is mechanics, and stays out.

## Proposal

No new syntax. Reading rules for constructs that already exist, one continuation rule, three
informational diagnostics, one Architect conduct rule, and a wording fix in §18.

### 1. §7.3 — the step's opening Persona (new bullet in *Step grammar (5.3)*, after "A step with no edges is a human step")

> - **A step's opening Persona is its performer (5.9).** "The Manager reviews the evidence —
>   [invokes](aim:#Contract:Review)." When a step's text begins with a declared Persona's name, or an
>   `Imports` alias of one (§9.1), optionally after an article, that Persona performs the step and the
>   operations it invokes, unless an operation has a direct performer of its own (§7.4). A Persona named
>   later in the sentence is someone the step concerns, never its doer ("Send the invoice to the
>   Client"), and a possessive never names a doer ("The Clerk's manager signs"). A step that opens with
>   no Persona inherits the Flow's performer. The opening Persona does not carry across an exclusive
>   step's alternatives: each alternative keeps its own performer.

### 2. §7.3 — continuation through an announcement (new paragraph after *Continuation after a decision (5.5)*)

> **Continuation through an announcement (5.9).** Only `invokes` and `emits` move a path on from a step
> or an outcome; `reads`, `mutates` and `satisfies` never do. An `emits` continues the path at the
> Event's subscribers. A subscriber without `### Steps` of its own — typically a Contract — is the
> path's next work. A subscriber with steps of its own — a Flow, or a promoted process — starts a run of
> its own and does not continue this one. An Event nothing subscribes to ends the path at that point; a
> Trigger anchored on it (§7.7) arms a timer and does not continue the path.

### 3. §7.4 — who does the work (new paragraph after "Entity and role are two things wearing one facet")

> **Who does the work (5.9).** Performing, being permitted and being able to reach are three different
> facts, and the model states each in its own place. A piece of work — an operation, or a step of a
> Flow or promoted process — is **performed** by the first of these that applies:
>
> 1. **The Persona that `invokes` it** (§8.2) — its direct performer.
> 2. **The Persona its step opens with** (§7.3) — for the step itself, and for the operations the step
>    invokes that have no direct performer.
> 3. **The performer of the enclosing Flow** — work with no performer of its own inherits the nearest
>    enclosing Flow's (or promoted process's) performer, which is that Flow's direct performer or what
>    it inherited in turn. The first declaration stops the inheritance.
> 4. **The flow itself** — a step whose only operations are `reads`, `mutates` or `emits` is work the
>    flow performs (§7.3). It owes no Persona.
>
> None of the following is evidence of who performs work:
>
> - **`### Authz`** states who *may* — permission. Work can be permitted to three roles and performed by
>   one. Where Authz names roles, the performer MUST be among them; Authz never names the performer.
> - **Reach.** A Persona that `accesses` a View exposing an operation can get to it; that is not a
>   commitment that they do it.
> - **A Trigger** starts work (§7.7); it never does it. Work a schedule or deadline starts still owes a
>   performer.
> - **`### Role` prose** describes the actor, not an assignment of any particular work.
>
> Work is **owed** a performer when something obliges it to happen: it is a step of a Flow or promoted
> process, work a step invokes, or work a Trigger or a subscription starts. Owed work with no performer
> by rules 1–4 has **no declared performer**, and the model is incomplete at that point (§12.2). Work
> that is only offered — an operation a View exposes, which whoever reaches it may do within its Authz —
> owes no performer.

### 4. §12.2 — three informational diagnostics (append)

> - **No declared performer (5.9)** — owed work (§7.4) with no performer by §7.4's rules: nothing names
>   one; or only `### Authz` names who may, and permission is not performance; or only a Trigger starts
>   it, and a timer is not a doer. Tools SHOULD say which of the three applies, and SHOULD ask once of a
>   Flow when several of its steps lack the same answer, since one declaration on the Flow answers every
>   step that inherits from it. Remediation: the performing Persona `invokes` the work, or the step opens
>   with it. Only the owner can answer this; the Architect never assigns a performer to close it (§1.2).
> - **Performer conflict (5.9)** — the performer the model declares for a piece of work is not among the
>   roles its `### Authz` permits; or Authz names several roles without saying whether any one of them
>   suffices, and nothing declares which performs it. The model either says two things or leaves "all
>   of them, or any one?" open. Remediation: align performer and permission, write the alternative
>   ("Manager or Director"), or make separate steps when all must act.
> - **Role without work (5.9)** — a declared Persona that performs nothing, reaches nothing, and is named
>   in no step and no `### Authz` (§7.4). Remediation: give the role its part, or remove it.

### 5. §1.2 — Architect conduct (amend *Architect: validate before present*)

Current: *"Only diagnostics that genuinely need a human decision are surfaced: a confirmed-duplicate
`merge` (§16.3 invariant 6), an unresolved canonical-home choice (§15.8), or an ambiguous binding
(§12.3)."*

Proposed:

> Only diagnostics that genuinely need a human decision are surfaced: a confirmed-duplicate `merge`
> (§16.3 invariant 6), an unresolved canonical-home choice (§15.8), an ambiguous binding (§12.3), or a
> missing or conflicting performer (§7.4, §12.2). Who does the work is the owner's commitment; the
> Architect never assigns a performer to make a diagnostic go away.

### 6. §18 — wording (amend *Do not mirror the org chart*)

Current: *"the hierarchy belongs in the model as **Personas and `### Authz`** ("Accountant may…", "CFO
approves…"), never as namespace segments."*

Proposed:

> the hierarchy belongs in the model as **Personas, their `### Authz`, and who performs what** — "the
> Accountant may post…" is permission, in Authz; "The CFO approves the budget" is performance, written
> as a step's opening Persona or an `invokes` (§7.4) — never as namespace segments.

## Against §8.6

The five tests are written for constructs, and this proposal adds none. Run anyway:

1. **Relational commitment?** The rules read relations the graph already holds (`invokes`, step
   membership, Flow nesting). The step-subject rule reads a position inside an existing structured
   list — the same kind of rule as "indented continuation lines belong to the step above them".
2. **Not expressible today?** Direct performance is. A human step's performer is not, and inheritance
   and Authz's standing are unspecified — which is why two readers in one codebase disagree.
3. **Pays rent?** Swimlanes, "whose turn is it" in a guided case, per-persona prompt export, and the
   three diagnostics are computable only once the rules are fixed. The reference implementation's
   process check pins them with its test suite and a parity golden.
4. **Demonstrated need?** The 5.4 open item; the owner's review rules for the process check
   (2026-09-25: permission is not performance, a timer is not a doer); the two-reader disagreement above.
5. **Fully specified?** Precedence, stops, non-evidence, the owed/offered line, three diagnostics.

## Consequences for the reference implementation, if adopted as written

- **The guided runner changes.** It routes work by the declared performer, and Authz becomes the check
  that the performer is permitted. Where no performer is declared it may still offer the work to the
  permitted roles — a routing choice the Realizer makes and surfaces (§1.2) — but it reports *No declared
  performer* instead of treating the fallback as the model's answer. The step-subject rule and the
  possessive rule apply to guided cases too.
- **Models where Authz and `invokes` agree behave identically.** All three shipped templates do.
- **The process check needs little change:** its codes map onto the three diagnostics (`NO_RESPONSIBLE_ROLE`,
  `ONLY_PERMITTED` and `STARTED_BY_TIMER_NO_DOER` are the three reasons for *No declared performer*;
  `PERFORMER_UNCLEAR` is *Performer conflict*; `ROLE_WITHOUT_WORK` is *Role without work*). Today it runs
  for Business Processes only; the spec makes the rules apply to owed work in any model.
- **The prompt export** still carries its own copy of the continuation rule; it should read the shared one.
- Re-run the process and approval benchmarks.

## What this does not settle

- **Unheard announcements.** The reference process check also asks about every emitted Event nothing
  consumes — Unhandled outcome (5.4) extended beyond decision outcomes. Applications routinely announce
  to consumers outside the model, so this wants evidence from application models before it goes into
  §12.2.
- **Loops and deadlines.** The process check asks whether a deadline touching a loop ends it ("a deadline
  is not a way out"), while §15.7 names a deadline Trigger as the commitment-shaped bound on a correction
  loop. The two agree only when the model says what happens to the loop when the deadline fires. Worth
  its own look.
- **Joint performance** ("the Manager and the Director both sign"). Expressible today as two steps; no
  new form proposed.
