# Proposal (AIM 6.6) — a comparison between two fields, or a field and the clock

Status: **written into the specification (AIM 6.6), 2026-11-15** — drafted 2026-11-15, revised the same day after two reviews (kept at the end; "What the revision changed" lists the changes); the eleven decisions accepted as drafted · Author: Michael & Claude working session · Would affect: §3 (Markdown restrictions: one new fence), §6.1 (Record: `### Constraints`), §6.2 (Operation: `### Criteria`, `### Expects`, the write rule), §6.3 (Process: Criteria and Expects as on an Operation), §6.16 (Output: `### Constraints`, one wording fix), §9 (impact set), §10 (diagnostics)

## Where this comes from

After 6.5, all six example models under Amy's `examples/` (airline-operations, club, e-commerce, german-government, hotel-management, nasa) were read in full for commitments that only prose carries. One finding recurred in every one of them: a rule that compares two fields, or a field with the clock, cannot be written in a form a tool can check. The owner raised the same gap with a case of his own:

- **A vacation request.** "Not more days than you have left." The days asked for are an Input; the days left are a field of the employee's row. The rule compares the two.

The clearest lines from the examples, with file and line (paths under `examples/`):

1. **Clock against a field.** `hotel-management/aim/hotel/stay/hotel.stay.aim:455`: "**FreeOfCharge** — the guest cancels before the reservation's free-cancellation deadline". The Input is `reservationId` alone; the deadline is `Reservation.cancellationDeadline` (`hotel.aim:116`). The decision has no Criteria.
2. **Field against fields of another row.** `nasa/aim/nasa/jsc/mcc/telemetry/nasa.jsc.mcc.telemetry.aim:94`: "**Within** — the value lies between the measurement's low and high limits". The Input is `frameId` alone.
3. **Computed outside the model.** `airline-operations/aim/airline/departure/airline.departure.aim:464`: `slotOffsetMinutes: integer optional label("Expected take-off against the slot, in minutes")`, tested at line 485 (`Cleared when slotOffsetMinutes between -5 and 10`), while `slotAt` is stored at line 73. `club/aim/tsv/aufnahme/tsv.aufnahme.aim:115` does the same with `alter`, tested at line 136 (`Jugend when alter < 18`), beside a stored `geburtsdatum`.
4. **Period counted from a field, no Trigger.** `german-government/aim/bundesregierung/legislation/secondpassage/bundesregierung.legislation.secondpassage.aim:76`: "The request is within three weeks of bundesratDecisionReceivedOn", and line 289: "lodges no objection within two weeks of objectionPeriodStartsOn". That file holds no Trigger and no Criteria.
5. **Counting rule in a Summary.** `e-commerce/aim/shop/orders/shop.orders.aim:499`: "The days count as the law counts them: from the day after the parcel arrived to the end of the fourteenth day", beside `14 days after [shop.orders#Event:ParcelDelivered]` (line 507). The Test at line 28 depends on it. NASA stores `Mission.launchWindowCloses` (`nasa.esdmd.artemis3.aim:46`) while its Trigger counts `3 hours after [CountdownStarted]` (`countdown.aim:331`) and `Launch` expects "the window is open" (line 260).
6. **Derived values.** `hotel-management/aim/hotel/hotel.aim:124-126`: "The departure is later than the arrival.", "The nights are every date from the arrival to the day before the departure.", "Free cancellation ends at 18:00 two days before the arrival." `invoicing/aim/invoicing/invoicing.aim:101`: "`totalAmount` equals the sum of `lineTotal` across `lines`."

## The problem

Three places in 6.5 cause it.

- **§6.2, Criteria.** "The inputs are the operation's Input fields and nothing else. … values are literals of the input's type". The same paragraph names the way out: "A judgement the rules cannot express is made first, by an earlier human or automated operation, and arrives as an Input the rules read." With `slotOffsetMinutes` a tool checks −5 and 10; nobody checks the subtraction or which two times went into it.
- **§6.2, Expects.** "Preconditions, one bullet each." Criteria exist only beside `### Decides`. The vacation rule is a precondition, not a decision, so it has no checkable place at all.
- **§6.1, Constraints.** "Data constraints, one bullet each." §6.2's write rule holds a mutation to "the Record's `### Schema` and `### Immutable`, ownership … and every State governing the Record" and does not name Constraints. Elsewhere the text is stronger than the form allows: a part "follows … the Record's `### Constraints`" (§3), and §6.16 says "A rule that can be stated on the content belongs in Contains or Constraints, where it is checked." No form exists that a tool could check; a realization can only judge the prose.

Two further places bear on the neighbouring gaps (Part "Not in this proposal"):

- **§6.7, Schedule.** "`<n> <unit> after [<intent>#Event:<Name>]` with `minutes`, `hours`, `days`, `business-days` or `weeks`". A deadline counts from an Event only.
- **§6.3, a timed wait.** A step may already wait `— until [<address>]` on a `date` or `datetime` field of the case's record. So a field can anchor time in one place, without an offset and without a deadline's powers (`unless`, `### Ends`, `triggers`).

**6.4 left this open on purpose.** `6.4-flow-constructs.md`, Part 5: "Inputs are the operation's Input fields. Nothing else in this version: a read Record names a kind of row, not a row, so `Account.balance` says nothing when the operation reads two accounts." Under "What is deliberately not filled": "Criteria over Record fields or Capability results: when an input can name a row." And the line it drew: "no expressions beyond a comparison, no functions, nothing a parser cannot check against the schema."

## What is one problem and what is separate

- **Comparing two fields and comparing with the clock are one problem**: what the two sides of a comparison may be. Today the left is an Input field and the right a literal. Both cases need a second kind of operand and nothing else.
- **Where a comparison may be written is the second half of the same problem.** A decision (Criteria), a precondition (Expects) and a rule on a row (Constraints) hold the same comparison; only Criteria has a checkable form.
- **A deadline anchored on a field is a separate construct.** It is a timer (§6.7), with its own questions: a field that changes after arming, a field with no value. 6.4 answered both for `until`. Units beyond weeks and the counting rule belong with it, because months need an end-of-month rule and one answer must serve the timer and the comparison alike.
- **A value derived from other values is separate and the expensive part.** A sum, a product, "two days before at 18:00" need arithmetic and functions, which 6.4 ruled out by name.

**Recommended: A now.** It is the only step that stays inside the line 6.4 drew, and it is the precondition 6.4 itself named: an Input declared `ref(...)` names exactly one row. The field-anchored deadline with its counting rule follows as its own proposal. Derived values stay prose.

## Proposal (draft of A)

### Part 1 — operands

A comparison is `<left> <op> <right>`, with the operators §6.2 already lists, or `<operand> present` / `<operand> absent`. What may stand on each side widens:

- **A literal**, on the right only, as today. A literal never stands on the left: a bare word there is a field name, so a misspelled field is an error and not a silent comparison of two values.
- **A field of this place**, by its bare name on the left, as today, and in brackets on the right: `[tageUebrig]`. The brackets are required on the right, so that a literal and a field never collide: `status = open` compares with the value `open`, `status = [open]` with a field named `open`. On the left the brackets are allowed and mean the same: `[departure] > [arrival]`. Every rule written under 6.4 and 6.5 keeps its meaning.
- **A field of the row an Input names**: `[<input>.<field>]`. `<input>` is an Input field declared `ref(...)` whose target field is declared `unique`, so it names exactly one row, and `<field>` is a field of the Record it references. `<input>` may also be an Input declared `part(<Record>)`; the value is then in hand and no row is looked up. It may stand on either side. One step; no longer path in this version.
- **The clock**: `now` beside a `datetime`, `today` beside a `date`. `now` is an instant and needs no timezone; `today` is the date in the `timezone` in reach (§6.7), and without one a realization MUST NOT evaluate it. One evaluation reads the clock once.

Every comparison names at least one field. `5 > 3` and `now = now` are errors.

**The clock words keep every existing meaning.** On the left, an Input field named `now` or `today` is that field, as it is today, and a tool says so; the clock is meant only where no such field exists. On the right, a bare `now` beside a `datetime` field, or `today` beside a `date` field, is not a valid literal today, so no valid 6.5 rule changes; beside an enum or a string it stays the literal it was.

**Types.** Both sides MUST be of one type.

- `integer`, `number` and `decimal` compare with one another, exactly. `date` compares with `date` and `datetime` with `datetime`, never one with the other. Two `datetime` values compare by instant, whatever offset each was written with.
- `<`, `<=`, `>`, `>=` and `between` apply to numbers, `date` and `datetime`, and to nothing else.
- An enum compares with a literal of its values, or with a field whose enum has the same set of values. Enums are declared inline (§3), so "the same enum" means the same values.
- Two `ref(...)` fields compare with `=` and `!=` only when they reference the same field of the same Record: `reviewerId != [ownerId]`.
- `between a and b` and `in (...)` take operands in the same way.

**An absent value.** `present` and `absent` are always evaluated; they are how a rule asks. For any other comparison:

- In **Criteria** it does not hold, as today for an absent Input.
- In an **Expects** check, the line is not applied when an optional Input field of the operation itself is absent: the caller left it out, as with `min` and `max`. When a field read through `[<input>.<field>]` is absent, from the row a `ref` names or from a part that was given (decided 2026-11-15, after the specification was written), the line does not hold and the operation is refused: a precondition never passes because the value it needs is missing.
- In a **Constraints** check the line is not applied. `closedAt >= [openedAt]` says nothing while `closedAt` is empty.

### Part 2 — the three places

All three places exist in their promoted forms too (`## Expects`, `## Constraints`, `## Criteria`; §6.1, §6.3, §6.16), with the same grammar.

**Criteria (§6.2, §6.3).** The rule line stays `<Outcome> when <comparison>[, <comparison>]...`. Its inputs are the operation's Input fields and the rows its `ref` Inputs name.

```criteria
FreeOfCharge when now < [reservationId.cancellationDeadline]
otherwise WithFee
```

**Expects (§6.2, §6.3).** `### Expects` MAY hold one `check` fence, alone or beside bullets, with exactly one comparison per line; every line must hold. Operands are as in Criteria. A realization MUST refuse the operation when a line does not hold, before it performs anything, and says which line. It evaluates the check only after `### Authz` and the ownership rules of §3 have passed, so a refusal never tells a caller about a row the caller may not read. The bullets are further preconditions, judged as today; a bullet that is written as a check line is replaced by it, not kept beside it.

````markdown
### Input

```schema
mitarbeiterId: string required ref(firma#Mitarbeiter.id)
tageBeantragt: integer required min(1)
```

### Expects

```check
tageBeantragt <= [mitarbeiterId.tageUebrig]
```

### Ensures

- The days asked for are taken off the employee's days left — [mutates](aim:firma#Record:Mitarbeiter)
````

The Ensures line belongs to the example: the check alone compares, and only the write that takes the days off keeps a second request from using them again (Part 3).

On a **Process** the Expects check is evaluated when the case starts, before the first step. A Process runs for days and holds nothing in between; a value that must still hold at the moment of a write is guarded by the Operation that writes, or by the Record's Constraints. A Process's Criteria read rows and the clock when the decision is reached, after its steps.

**Constraints (§6.1, §6.16).** `### Constraints` MAY hold one `check` fence, alone or beside bullets, one comparison per line. A block that holds only a fence is not empty. Operands are the fields of the Record itself, or of an Output's Contains.

- **On a Record**, every create and every update MUST satisfy every line before it commits, and the write rule of §6.2 names them beside Schema and Immutable. The lines are evaluated on the row as it will stand: on a create after `default(...)` values are filled, on an update with the written fields laid over the stored ones. A delete evaluates nothing. The clock MUST NOT appear: a rule that is true only at the moment of writing is a precondition of the operation that writes and belongs in its Expects. `arrival >= today` on a Reservation would refuse the check-out the day after arrival.
- **On an Output**, the content is held to the lines once, when it is handed over (§6.16). An Output is never changed afterwards, so the clock is allowed there.
- **A part** is held to its Record's lines wherever its value rules are checked (§3).

````markdown
### Constraints

```check
departure > [arrival]
```

- The nights are every date from the arrival to the day before the departure.
````

### Part 3 — rules

- **Checked and judged are kept apart.** A line in a `check` or `criteria` fence is evaluated, never judged. A prose bullet is judged, never claimed as proven. §6.16's sentence becomes: a rule that can be stated as a comparison on the content belongs in a `check` fence, where it is checked.
- **The values are those of the moment.** One evaluation reads one consistent set of row values and the clock once. On an Operation, the values a check read and the writes the Operation makes commit as one: no other write to those values falls in between.
- **A comparison guards; it does not reserve.** Two vacation requests cannot use the same days only because the operation that passes the check also takes the days off the balance, in the same act. That is the operation's Ensures and stays its commitment. A check on an operation that changes nothing it compared with protects nothing against the next caller.
- **A value that follows from others stays prose.** Whether `tageUebrig` is right is the commitment of the operation that maintains it. The comparison is checked against the stored field.
- **A derived reference, not an edge.** A rule that names a field derives a structured reference from its host to that field, outside the closed taxonomy of §7, like a State's moves. A changed field puts every rule that names it in the impact set (§9).
- **Kept and rewritten like everything else.** Criteria and `check` fences join the list under Preservation of meaning (§3; 6.4 left Criteria out of it): parsing and serializing keep every line. A transform that renames a Record field or an Input field (§12) rewrites `[<field>]` and `[<input>.<field>]` in every rule that names it.
- **No new verb, no new facet, no arithmetic.** A point in time a rule compares with is a stored field, as `cancellationDeadline` already is.

### Part 4 — diagnostics

Hard error: an operand that is neither a literal of the other side's type, a field of the place, a field reached through a `ref` or `part` Input, `now` or `today`; a literal on the left; a comparison that names no field; `[<input>.<field>]` where the Input is neither a `ref(...)` nor a `part(...)`, where the `ref` target is not declared `unique`, or where the field is not in the referenced Record; two sides of unlike types; an ordering operator on a type that has no order; two `ref` fields compared that reference different targets; `now` beside anything but a `datetime`, `today` beside anything but a `date`; the clock in a Record's Constraints (name the writing operation's Expects); a `check` fence anywhere but Expects and Constraints; two `check` fences in one block; a `check` line with more than one comparison; a `check` line naming an outcome.

Informational: a rule that reads a row's field while its operation declares neither `reads` nor `mutates` of that Record; a comparison whose two operands are the same operand (two different `ref` Inputs to one Record are not: `[fromAccountId.currency] = [toAccountId.currency]`); a `check` line no value can satisfy; `today` with no `timezone` in reach (ask which); an Input field named `now` or `today` in an operation whose rules use the word (say that the field is meant); an Expects check on an operation that mutates no Record the check reads (ask whether the value should be reserved).

## What the examples would then say

- **Vacation request**: the Expects check above.
- **Hotel cancellation**: the Criteria above; the Reservation's first Constraint becomes a check, the other two stay prose.
- **Club fee class**: `alter` stays an Input. Age is date arithmetic, which this proposal does not add.
- **Airline slot**: `slotOffsetMinutes` goes only if the window's two bounds are stored fields; then `Cleared when [turnaroundId.expectedTakeOffAt] between [turnaroundId.slotWindowOpensAt] and [turnaroundId.slotWindowClosesAt]`.
- **Telemetry limits**: the frame and its limit are two rows, and today nothing links them: `TelemetryFrame.measurement` is a plain `string` (line 32), not a reference. The rule is writable when the operation takes both rows as `ref` Inputs, and it then needs a line that ties them, or the frame could be measured against another measurement's limits:

  ```check
  [frameId.measurement] = [limitId.measurement]
  ```

  With `frameId` alone it would need `measurement` declared as a reference to `Limit.measurement` (which is `unique`, line 48) and a second step through it, which this version leaves out.
- **German periods**: checkable only where the due date is a stored field. The period itself stays prose until the next proposal.

## Not in this proposal

- **A deadline anchored on a field, a period as an operand, months and years, and the counting rule** (B). Its own proposal, built on 6.4's decisions for `until`: resolved once and kept, an absent value waits for the write.
- **Derived fields** (C). An equation such as "the total equals the sum of its lines" states an outcome, not mechanics; it is left out for what it brings with it (aggregation, rounding, date functions), not because it would be foreign to AIM.
- **A path through more than one reference.**
- **A full order of checks for an Operation** (Authz, Input, State, Expects, Criteria, commit). This proposal fixes one point of it: an Expects check runs after Authz and ownership. 6.5 states no order; the rest is a question of its own.
- **Criteria over a Capability's result**, which 6.4 deferred together with Record fields.
- Two findings of the same reading: nothing names who receives an Output, and money fields carry no currency or tax basis. The second touches this proposal at one point: two `decimal` fields in different currencies compare as numbers.

## Existing models stay valid

Every 6.5 file is valid, unchanged. A right side without brackets is a literal, as it was. Criteria over Input fields mean what they meant, also where an Input is named `now` or `today` (Part 1). A `ref(...)` whose target is not `unique` stays valid; only reading a field through it is refused. `### Expects` and `### Constraints` without a fence are prose, judged as before. Nothing is deprecated and nothing is migrated.

## Decisions to confirm

The draft takes a position on each. All are the owner's.

1. **A first, B as its own proposal, C not now.**
2. **Brackets mark a field on the right side**, are allowed on the left, and a literal never stands on the left. The alternative, a bare name that is read as a field when one exists, changes the meaning of existing rules where an enum value and a field share a name.
3. **One step through a `ref` Input, and through a `part` Input.** The alternative, a path of any length, covers the telemetry case and makes "which row" harder to read.
4. **The fence is called `check`**, lives inside Expects and Constraints (promoted forms included), at most one per block, exactly one comparison per line. The alternative is a sub-block of its own.
5. **An absent operand.** Criteria: does not hold. Expects: not applied when the operation's own optional Input is absent, refused when a field read from a row is absent. Constraints: not applied. `present` and `absent` always evaluated. The simpler alternative is that every absent operand in Expects refuses; it costs the "if given" reading of an optional Input.
6. **Constraints checks join the write rule of §6.2**, on the resulting row, on create and update, not on delete. Prose Constraints stay judged, and the text says so.
7. **No clock in a Record's Constraints; allowed in an Output's.** The alternative (second review) allows it on a Record and states that a row can age past its own rule, after which an unrelated change is refused.
8. **`now` and `today` as the two words for the clock**, with an existing Input of that name winning. The alternative is a spelling no field can have, such as `@now`.
9. **A field is read only through a `ref` whose target is `unique`.** New after review.
10. **An Expects check runs after Authz and ownership.** New after review. The full order of checks is left out.
11. **On a Process, Expects is checked at case start and Criteria when the decision is reached.** New after review.

## Review feedback (2026-11-15)

Overall assessment: **Option A is well-scoped, well-evidenced, and stays cleanly inside 6.4's design boundary.** Deferring **B** (field-anchored deadlines, period offsets, counting rules) and **C** (derived formulas) keeps comparisons schema-checkable without smuggling calendar arithmetic or aggregation into the language, while fixing an existing contradiction in 6.5 (§3 holds parts to `### Constraints` and §6.16 says rules in `Constraints` are *"where it is checked"*, while §6.2's write rule omits `Constraints`).

Before writing **A** into the specification, eight execution and grammar edge cases should be resolved so two conforming realizations cannot follow the text and behave differently.

### Execution & semantic gaps

1. **Record `Constraints` on the clock (`now` / `today`) create a lifecycle-brick trap (Decision 7).**
   Under *"Every write to a row MUST satisfy every line before it commits"*, if `Record: Reservation` has `arrival >= today` in `### Constraints`:
   - Booking on Monday for Friday succeeds (`Friday >= Monday`).
   - Checking out on Saturday mutates `Reservation.status` — and is **refused** because `Friday >= Saturday` is false. Every subsequent status transition, update, or (since `mutates` in §7 includes delete) deletion is bricked once the timestamp passes.
   - "Arrival must not be in the past when booked" is an `### Expects` precondition on `BookReservation`, not a timeless row invariant. By contrast, an **Output** is never kept or later updated (§6.16), so a clock check on Output `Constraints` runs once at handover/acceptance and bricks nothing.
   - *Recommendation:* Either **forbid `now` / `today` in Record `### Constraints`** (with a diagnostic directing the author to `### Expects` on the creating operation) while allowing it in `Criteria`, `Expects`, and Output `Constraints`; or restrict Record `Constraints` explicitly to **create and update (never delete)** and warn strongly that *any* later update after the time passes will be refused.

2. **Absent operands in `Expects` when a *referenced row's* field is optional (Decision 5).**
   - On **Record / Output `Constraints`**, skipping a value comparison when an optional operand is absent matches SQL `CHECK` (`closedAt >= [openedAt]` passes while `closedAt` is absent).
   - On **`Expects` with `[<input>.<field>]`**, Input `required` only requires `<input>` (the reference), **not** `<field>` on the target row. If `Mission.windowClosesAt` or `Customer.creditLimit` is `optional` and absent (`null`), skipping `now < [missionId.windowClosesAt]` or `amount <= [customerId.creditLimit]` silently passes the precondition.
   - Also, §6.2's operator list already includes unary **`present` and `absent`**. `[orderId.shippedAt] absent` is a common precondition; Part 1 should keep unary `<operand> present` / `<operand> absent` explicitly, and those operators must never be skipped when the operand is absent.
   - *Recommendation:* Keep unary `present` / `absent` active everywhere; keep skip-on-absent for `Constraints` (and optional direct `Input` fields); and in `Expects`, **refuse** (or at minimum emit an informational diagnostic) when an unguarded value comparison reads an `optional` field on a referenced row `[<input>.<field>]` whose value is absent.

3. **Process (`§6.3`) `Expects` and `Criteria` timing vs. atomicity.**
   - An **Operation** is atomic, so its `Expects` check and `Ensures` write can commit together.
   - A **Process** spans human steps, waits, and weeks; its `Expects` cannot hold a transaction lock until a step days later mutates a balance. And unlike 6.4 (where `Criteria` read immutable `Input` fields only), a Process's `Criteria` can now read `[<input>.<field>]` and `now`, which change while the steps run.
   - *Recommendation:* State explicitly that on a Process: (a) `Expects` is evaluated **when the case starts**, before step 1, while write-time atomicity against concurrent cases belongs to the mutating Operation (or Record `Constraints`); and (b) `Criteria` is evaluated **when the steps complete** to reach the process's decision, against the row values and clock at that moment.

4. **Check order & cross-owner probing in `Expects`.**
   *"A realization MUST refuse the operation when a line does not hold, before it performs anything, and says which line."* If `Expects` runs before `Authz` and row ownership (`owner`, `!owner`, `cross-owner`), an unauthorized caller can pass another actor's `mitarbeiterId` and probe `tageUebrig` via the *"says which line"* refusal.
   - *Recommendation:* Fix the check order in §6.2: **(1) `Authz` → (2) `Input` schema validity (`required`, types, `min`/`max`, `ref` existence, parts' `Constraints`) & row ownership scope (§3) → (3) State `Only in` (§6.10) and `Expects` `check` → (4) `Criteria` / `Ensures` / Record `Constraints` & State `Moves` at commit.**

5. **Record `Constraints` on partial updates and defaults.**
   When an Operation creates a row or updates a subset of fields (e.g. updating `arrival` without rewriting `departure`), `Constraints` MUST be evaluated on the **resulting post-write row** (on create: after `default(...)` values in §3 are filled; on update: written fields merged over existing stored fields; on delete: not evaluated).

### Grammar, types & diagnostics

6. **Operand, operator, and type boundaries (Part 1).**
   - **No literals on the left:** Part 1 says *"What may stand on each side widens: A literal, as today."* In 6.5 a literal cannot stand on the left. Because bare enum values and unquoted strings (`open`, `Green`) share the same token shape as bare left-hand field names, allowing literals on the left turns a misspelled field (`typo = [status]`) into a string literal comparison. Restrict `<left>` to: **a local field (bare, or optionally `[field]`), `[<input>.<field>]`, or `now` / `today`**, and require **at least one side to be a field** (ruling out `5 > 3` and `now = now`).
   - **Optional `[field]` brackets on the left:** Since `[turnaroundId.slotAt]` uses brackets on the left and `[arrival]` uses brackets on the right, authors and tools will write `[departure] > [arrival]`. Accepting optional brackets on a local left-hand field (while requiring them on the right) avoids a needless syntax trap.
   - **Reserve `now` and `today`:** State that bare `now` and `today` always denote the clock (a field named `today` would be written `[today]`, or warn on naming a field `now` / `today`). Note also that `now` compares absolute UTC instants against `datetime` (timezone-independent), whereas `today` requires a timezone — define runtime behavior for `today` when `timezone` is omitted (since missing `timezone` is informational, not a hard error).
   - **Ordering operators (`<`, `<=`, `>`, `>=`, `between`):** Restrict to `integer` / `number` / `decimal`, `date`, and `datetime` (hard error on `boolean`, `enum`, `email`, `url`, `phone`, `file`, `ref`, `part`, `list`, `map`). State explicitly that `datetime` values with different UTC offsets compare **by instant** (not ISO string order), and `between` applies to `date` and `datetime` as well as numbers.
   - ***"The same enum"*:** AIM has no nominal enum definitions — every `enum(...)` is inline on its field (§3). Define *"the same enum"* structurally as **the same set of values**.
   - **Two `ref(...)` fields:** Two `ref` fields targeting **different** Records (`ref(team#Member.id)` vs `ref(shop#Product.id)`) should be a type hard error even when both underlying scalars are `string`, whereas two `ref`s to the same Record field (`reviewerId != [ownerId]`) compare as equal/unequal.

7. **Three wording/diagnostic fixes (Parts 2 & 4).**
   - **Contradiction in `check` line grammar (Part 2):** *"one comparison per line; the conditions of one line hold together, and every line must hold."* Since every line already holds together (`AND`), comma-joined conditions on a single line contradict *"one comparison per line"* and blur *"says which line"* on refusal. Make `check` strictly **one comparison per line**, with at most one `check` fence per `Expects` or `Constraints` block.
   - **False-positive `reads` diagnostic (Part 4):** *"a rule that reads a row's field while its operation declares no `reads` of that Record"* will misfire on nearly every mutating operation (`ApproveClaim`, `CancelReservation`), which declare `[mutates](aim:...)` without a separate `[reads](aim:...)` (cf. *"declared `reads` or `mutates` targets"* in §3). Change to: *"declares **neither `reads` nor `mutates`** of that Record"*.
   - **Self-comparison diagnostic (Part 4):** *"a comparison of a field with itself"* must check **operand identity**, not `<Record>.<field>`, so `[fromAccountId.currency] = [toAccountId.currency]` (two distinct `ref` Inputs to `Account`) is not flagged.

8. **Promoted forms, parts, coexistence with prose, and evolution (§3, §6.1, §6.3, §6.16, §12).**
   - **Promoted forms:** Include top-level `## Expects` and `## Constraints` alongside `### Expects` and `### Constraints`, and ensure `ref(<intent>#<field>)` (promoted Record entities, §3) works identically in `[<input>.<field>]`.
   - **Prose + `check` coexistence:** Clarify that a `check` fence may stand **alone** (zero prose bullets, as in the vacation example; §6.16's informational *"an Output with no Constraints and no Ensures"* must treat a `check`-only `Constraints` block as non-empty) or beside prose bullets, and that unlike `### Decides` + `### Criteria`, prose bullets in `Expects` and `Constraints` are **additive** — authors replace a prose bullet when encoding it in `check` rather than keeping both.
   - **`part(...)` Inputs (Decision 3):** Keep `ref` traversal to 1 hop, and consider also allowing `[<input>.<field>]` when `<input>` is `part(<Record>)` (an in-hand single value with no join or multi-row ambiguity).
   - **Preservation & transforms (§3, §12):** Add `Criteria` and `check` fences to *Preservation of meaning* in §3 (6.4 omitted `Criteria` there), and state in §12 that renaming a Record or Input field rewrites `[<field>]` and `[<input>.<field>]`.

## Codex review feedback (2026-11-15)

**A is a worthwhile next step, but this draft should be revised before adoption as AIM 6.6.** It addresses a real gap, fits the existing facets, and sensibly separates comparisons from timers and derived values. This is additional review feedback, not an amendment to the draft or a resolution of the decisions above.

The main issues are semantic rather than syntactic:

1. **Skipping absent operands can bypass preconditions (Decision 5).**
   The proposed absence rule lets an Expects check disappear when a compared value is missing. `required` does not fully solve this: a field can legitimately be optional generally but necessary for one operation. At minimum, Expects should refuse when a comparison needs an absent value. Also define `present` and `absent` separately; those predicates must still run when their operand is absent.

2. **A `ref(...)` does not yet establish an unambiguous lookup (Part 1).**
   The operand definition assumes a reference names exactly one row. However, §3's generic reference rules do not require the target field to be `unique`; the map-key rules explicitly do. If several employees share a referenced value, whose balance is read? Require dereferenced references to identify exactly one row, preferably through a declared unique key. Apply this restriction to the new dereferencing construct without invalidating existing references.

3. **The concurrency guarantee exceeds what the comparison establishes (Part 3).**
   "A check and the write ... commit together" is insufficient to guarantee that two vacation requests cannot consume the same balance. Even sequential requests can both pass if each merely creates a request and leaves the balance unchanged. The example needs a commitment to reserve or debit the allowance in the same atomic action. Concurrent evaluation must also protect the values read, rather than merely group the check and write together. Distinguish this from a Process's entry precondition: a long-running Process cannot hold one transaction throughout its work.

4. **Bare clock names undermine the compatibility claim (Part 1, Decision 8).**
   `now` and `today` can already be Input names or literal enum values. For example, an Input named `now` with type `datetime` makes `now < [reservationId.cancellationDeadline]` ambiguous. Amy's current Criteria evaluator was checked and accepts both a `now` Input and a `today` enum literal. Choose distinguishable clock syntax or specify resolution that preserves every existing interpretation. This differs from the earlier review's recommendation to reserve bare `now` and `today`: reservation must not silently reinterpret existing rules.

5. **Evaluation needs a more complete contract (Parts 1 and 2).**
   The type rules and Constraints rules leave several implementation choices open:
   - Validate an update against the complete resulting row, including unchanged fields.
   - Use a consistent set of row values and one clock sample per evaluation.
   - Define equality and ordering for each supported type, including exact decimal comparisons and datetime comparisons by instant.
   - Define what "the same enum" means; AIM currently declares enums inline.
   - Cover unary `present`/`absent` explicitly; they do not fit `<left> <op> <right>`.

   These can remain compact rules. They should not be left to each realization.

6. **The telemetry example needs a relationship check.**
   The example discussion says the existing model needs another step through a reference. In fact, `TelemetryFrame.measurement` is plain text, not a reference to `Limit`. Adding `limitId` also requires checking that the supplied limit belongs to the frame's measurement:

   ```check
   [frameId.measurement] = [limitId.measurement]
   ```

   Otherwise, the numeric comparison could use an unrelated measurement's limits.

The graph approach is appropriate: structured dependencies need no new behavioral verb. However, the dependency rule should also cover preservation and transforms (§3, §12). Renaming a referenced field must rewrite comparison operands, and parse/serialize cycles must preserve every check. Flagging affected rules alone is insufficient.

Two broader judgments:

- Allowing clocks in Record Constraints is defensible under the stated write-time semantics. Make the consequence explicit: a row may age past the condition, and an unrelated edit can then fail. This is a legitimate tradeoff rather than an automatic reason to prohibit the construct.
- Deferring C is sensible because of aggregation, precision, and date semantics. But an equation such as `totalAmount = sum(lines.lineTotal)` still states an outcome. Its being executable does not inherently make it implementation mechanics.

The strongest justification for A is the vacation precondition, hotel cancellation decision, and arrival/departure constraint. The six-model survey supports the wider problem; it should not imply that A resolves all six models' gaps.
