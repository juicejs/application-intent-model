# Proposal (AIM 5.11) — Style: the look is intent, and it is a facet

Status: **admitted in 5.11** — §7.8, §9.4, §12.1, §12.2, §11.3, §15.9, §8.6, §13.12 of the specification · Author: Michael & Claude working session, 2026-10-17 · Affects: §2.1, §3.6, §7, §8.6, §9.4, §11.3, §12, §15.9, §13 · Syntax: **one new facet heading, `## Style: <Name>`; no new edges, no new schema-line grammar**

## The problem

5.8 named a product's **visual identity** and put it beside the model, as supplied material: a token
summary under `content/style/`, owed by the graph, supplied by the owner, never written into `.aim`
(§9.4). The reasoning was the copy's: words are realization, so dress must be too.

A year of use in the reference implementation says otherwise. The owner's account of how the
material side grew, one request at a time: design and layout asks were put outside the model
("probably wrong"); content asks became a seed-data editor nobody understood; styling went to an
"Identity" tab, "which completes the confusion". The identity sat in a store the model could not see,
the owner could not find, and the tree did not show. And the test that should have decided its
place was never applied:

**The §1.4 test.** *Is X a commitment a Reviewer can hold a realization accountable to, or is it how
a realization meets one?* A product's words can change weekly without the product changing. Its
look cannot: a rebrand changes what the owner committed to, and it is reviewed as one. "The surfaces
read warm and flat, cream and dark brown, never corporate blue" is a commitment; a stylesheet is how
one realization meets it. Copy failed the test and stayed material. The look passes it.

## The design

**A facet, not a section.** Two shapes were on the table once the look was intent.

- A reserved section, `## Identity`, like `## Summary` or `## Requirements`. Rejected: sections are
  blocks an intent has exactly one of, and they are edge-inert prose. A look is a *thing* — it has a
  name, a product may have more than one across its pages, and tools and people need to point at it.
  That is what a facet is (§2.1).
- A facet, `## Style: <Name>`. Admitted. Addressable (`shop#Style:Warm`), visible in the tree and
  every projection like any node, authored and reviewed through the same transforms as everything
  else (§16).

**Not called Identity.** The word is taken: the spec uses *identity* for a Record's canonical
identity (§15.8) and a file's path identity (§4.4), and implementations use it for a Persona's
identity. A third meaning inside the language would collide, and outside it nobody reads "Identity"
as the look of a product. **Style** is the plain word, and the one the existing material channel
already used. *Brand* is narrower than a product's look; *Theme* is a developer's word.

**Grain: identity, never stylesheets.** This proposal moves the *place* of what 5.8 defined and
keeps its *grain*. The sub-blocks are the token families 5.8 named — `### Palette`, `### Type`,
`### Shape`, `### Mark` — each a list of `<token>: <value>` lines where the value is the owner's
words, an exact value, or both. Exact values are used verbatim; words are elaborated by the
realization in the idiom of its stack and its time. Tokens are open; a parser accepts any. Layout,
component styling and the stylesheets themselves remain the Realizer's (§15.9). All sub-blocks are
optional: a Style with only its `### Summary` — the look in one breath — is a Style.

**Scope follows the tree; no edge.** An intent declares at most one Style (a second is a hard
error — nothing in the model could choose). It applies to every View of that intent and every
intent beneath it; the nearest Style wins. A product has one look; a page-intent that departs from
it declares its own. A Style therefore declares no edges and nothing points at it — it is not an
orphan and takes no part in the traceability chain. A `wears` edge from a View to a Style is
recorded as pressure (§8.6): no real model has needed more than the nearest-Style rule.

**The mark is two things.** `- mark: Amy's portrait, a transparent cutout` is intent — it commits to
a mark and says what it is. The picture is a file, and a file is supplied material (§9.4), riding
under `content/assets/`. A Realizer with only the words knows what to source; one with the file uses
it verbatim.

**Absent Style keeps 5.8's asymmetry.** A View with no Style in reach reads *no style declared* —
informational. The realization makes a clean neutral choice and records it. Invented copy asserts
facts; invented dress is reversible.

## Migration

Additive. `content/style/` is retired as a channel. The tokens an owner supplied in the tool before
5.11 were typed by the owner, and typing them was the signature: a tool MAY move them into a
`## Style:` on the intent whose look they were — in the owner's words where the tokens had them,
exact values kept exact — recording the move in the file's history as a migration and keeping the
tokens as backup (owner's decision, 2026-10-17: no approval step, nothing deleted). Material recovered
from a re-encoded system (§17) is the other kind and still waits for the owner's confirmation.

## What this proposal does not do

It does not move screen copy or starting rows: both still fail the §1.4 test and stay supplied
material (§9.4). It does not admit `wears`. It does not define how a tool renders or edits a Style —
a form with swatches instead of a text reader is a projection choice, left to implementations, as
long as the file stays the authority and an edit goes through the ordinary change path.
