# Proposal (AIM 5.4) — Decisions are first-class

Status: **draft for review** · Author: Amy working session, 2026-07-28 · Affects: §7.2, §7.3, §8.x, §12.2

## The problem

**Decisions are the basis of every process.** "Are the financial statements accurate?", "Approve or
return?", "Are the receipts complete?" — a process's shape comes from its decisions, and its loops
exist *because* of them. Every notation a process owner has ever seen puts the decision at the
centre of the diagram.

AIM cannot express one. The specification is silent; the only written guidance lives in a tool's
process lens ("branch logic is Steps prose; a weighty decision becomes a Contract"). The
consequence is not cosmetic — **the graph cannot distinguish a decision from parallel work**:

```
Review  emits Confirmed → GenerateReports,  DiscrepanciesFound → Investigate    (either/or)
Draft   emits Prepared  → Review,           AuditLogged        → Archive        (both, always)
```

Same node types, same verbs, same fan-out. Demonstrated against the reference implementation: a
projection that marks the first as a branch necessarily marks the second one too, and no heuristic
can separate them because the distinguishing fact — *these outcomes are mutually exclusive* — is
not in the model. Three things are lost:

1. **Exclusivity.** Whether the outcomes are alternatives or concurrent.
2. **The outcome's name.** "Accurate" / "Inaccurate" — the YES and NO a reader looks for.
3. **The criteria.** What the judgment turns on. This has no home in the language at all, and it is
   the part an auditor asks about first.

A related ambiguity in the 5.3 step grammar has the same root: it says two `invokes` in one step are
"alternatives **or** joint work", leaving a reader (and a tool) unable to tell which.

## Non-goal: a gateway node

BPMN and flowcharts draw the decision as its own symbol because they model **token flow for an
execution engine** — the diamond exists to say which way the token goes. AIM models **commitments**,
and a decision here is not plumbing: it is a judgment somebody makes, against criteria, with
accountability. That makes it commitment-shaped — but it also means it already has a node.

**The deciding operation *is* the decision.** `ReviewFinancialStatements` is not a task that hands
off to a diamond; reviewing *is* deciding. Splitting them across two nodes would:

- separate the judgment from the `### Authz` that says who may make it — losing accountability at
  the exact point where accountability matters most;
- mint a node with no commitment of its own, needing its own orphan rules, edge legality, and
  addressing;
- duplicate one real-world act as two model nodes, which every §16 transform then has to keep in
  step.

So: decisions become first-class **on the operation that makes them**, consistent with the language's
own rule that an edge is declared once, at the acting node (§8.3).

## Proposal: `### Decides`

A Contract or Flow that reaches a judgment declares its outcomes in a `### Decides` sub-block:

````markdown
## Contract: ReviewFinancialStatements

### Summary

The reviewer checks the draft statements against the reconciliations.

### Authz

- InternalReviewer only.

### Decides

- **Accurate** — every balance ties to a reconciliation and each adjustment is supported
  — [emits](aim:#Event:FinancialStatementsConfirmed)
- **Inaccurate** — any balance is unexplained, or an adjustment lacks support
  — [emits](aim:#Event:DiscrepanciesFound)
````

Grammar, deliberately the same shape as the 5.3 step grammar so there is one thing to learn:

- **One bullet = one outcome.** The bolded name is the outcome's label (the YES/NO), following the
  §3.6 name grammar so it is addressable (`#Contract:ReviewFinancialStatements→Decides[Accurate]`).
- **The prose after the dash is the criteria** — what the judgment turns on. Normative: a realization
  must decide on this basis, and a Reviewer checks that it does.
- **The outcome's edge tokens ride in its bullet**, exactly as a step's do. Normally one `emits`;
  `invokes`/`mutates` are legal where an outcome does work directly.
- **The block asserts exclusivity and exhaustiveness.** That is what `### Decides` *means* — no extra
  marker. Two or more bullets required; one bullet is not a decision.

## What the graph gains

Edges are unchanged — every outcome's `emits` derives exactly as today. The addition is a derived
**exclusivity group** on the deciding node: the set of outcome labels, each with its events and its
criteria text. Nothing about resolution, verb legality, or addressing changes, so this is additive
with no migration (§13-style relabel not required).

Consequences that fall out for free:

- **Projections can draw a true decision** — a diamond with labelled legs — instead of guessing.
  Today's reference implementation marks a "the path splits here" fork and has to admit in its
  tooltip that it cannot tell alternatives from concurrent outcomes.
- **The 5.3 step ambiguity resolves.** A step whose operations come from a deciding contract's
  outcomes is exclusive; anything else is joint work. The "alternatives or joint work" wording can
  be replaced with a rule.
- **`### Steps` stay the procedure, not the branch.** A flow's steps remain a sequence; where it
  forks, the fork belongs to the deciding contract the step invokes. Sequencing and judgment stay
  separate concerns.

## Diagnostics this unlocks (§12.2)

- **Unhandled outcome** — an outcome whose event nothing consumes: the half-modelled YES/NO a
  flowchart author writes when the losing branch trails off the page. Today this is indistinguishable
  from an ordinary dead-end event.
- **Single-outcome decision** — a `### Decides` with one bullet: either it is not a decision, or an
  outcome is missing.
- **Criteria-free outcome** — a labelled outcome with no basis stated: the judgment is unauditable.
- **Undeclared branch (informational)** — an operation emitting two or more events whose consumers
  diverge, with no `### Decides`: probably a decision the author left implicit. This is precisely the
  case the current heuristic has to guess at, converted into a question for a human.

## Open questions for review

1. **Block name.** `### Decides` reads as the acting node's own voice, matching `### Ensures`.
   `### Outcomes` is more neutral but weaker about exclusivity.
2. **Non-exclusive outcome sets.** Is there a real need to declare "these outcomes are the complete
   set, and more than one may happen"? If so it is a second block, not a flag on this one — but no
   motivating case has appeared yet.
3. **Decisions with no operation.** "Over €1,000 goes to the CFO" is routing. It is expressible today
   as the routing operation's decision, or as a `## Requirements` policy. Confirm no third form is
   needed.
4. **Guard on the losing path.** When an outcome loops back (correction paths), does anything need to
   bound the retry, or does that remain `### On Error` territory?

## Related gaps, same neighbourhood

Worth resolving in one pass rather than three, since all touch a Flow's steps:

- **`### On Error` has no grammar** — a compensating `emits` under it derives an edge identical to a
  happy-path one, so failure paths cannot be projected separately.
- **A prose step has no performer** — the reference implementation infers it from neighbouring steps.
  For a process model, who performs a manual step is a commitment, not an inference.
- **A step is not bindable** — §10.2 binds intents and facets, so §18's "a step with no edge and no
  binding is the digitalization backlog" is not computable.
