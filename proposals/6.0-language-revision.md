# Proposal (AIM 6.0) — one compact language, and the four commitments it adds

Status: **admitted in 6.0** (2026-10-26) · Author: Michael & Claude working sessions, 2026-10-25/26 · Affects: the whole specification, which is rewritten and renumbered (thirteen sections) · Syntax: **one new edge verb `plays`; one new modifier `cross-owner(...)`; one new reserved move target `unchanged`; no removed construct**

## Compatibility

A model valid under 5.13 is valid 6.0 source and is read as it is, without migration (§2). 6.0 changes no existing meaning. Two 5.13 spellings stay readable and are no longer written:

- **The same-intent edge shorthand** (`invokes` `` `#Contract:CreateTask` ``) — tools read it as that link; authors write `[invokes](aim:#Contract:CreateTask)`; a tool that rewrites a file writes the link form. A backticked address with no declared verb right before it — an Authz role, a Dependencies target — is never an edge (§7).
- **Binding sidecars** (`kind: binding`, `## Bind:`) — tools read them and merge their locators into the node's inline `### Bindings` before a sidecar is retired (§§3, 9).

A tool that parses `.aim` into another form and writes it back must keep every node, requirement (labelled or not), edge, schema modifier, `## Dependencies`, binding and the authority metadata (`provenance`, `owner`, `status`, `approved_by`, `approved_at`, `tags`, `display`, `nature`) — and must not migrate a file when its intermediate form would drop any of them (§3, *Preservation of meaning*).

## What 6.0 adds

**1. A role played by a shared one — `plays` (§6, §7).** A process keeps its own roles apart — the Submitter and the Approver of a claim are two lanes, two sets of permissions — while both are employees of the organization. 5.13 could only say this by merging them into one Persona (erasing the handoff) or by naming a second canonical entity. Now each local role is its own Persona whose `### Role` carries `[plays](aim:org#Persona:Employee)`. It inherits the shared role's identity, and nothing else: no permission, no ownership scope. Evidence: a workspace with one HR role and every process needing "HR here" as its own performer (Amy, 2026-10-25).

**2. Ownership that reaches what it should, and no further (§3).** `owner` marks the field naming whose row it is; a row that references an owned row through `ref(...)` inherits that owner, through any chain — the line of a private claim is as private as the claim. Every applicable restriction must hold. `cross-owner(#Record:Claim)`, written after a backticked role in a Contract's `### Authz` or right after an `[accesses](aim:…)` edge in a Persona's `### Access`, widens scope for that one record, for that operation or that View — never for related records, never inherited. `!owner` forbids acting on one's own row; it grants nothing. "An approver approves other people's claims, never their own" is `` `#Persona:Approver` cross-owner(#Record:Claim) !owner ``. A misplaced or unresolved grant, or two `owner` fields on one Record, is a hard error (§10).

**3. A State may keep an operation to some states — `→ unchanged` (§6).** `- Draft → unchanged — [Contract:EditDraft]` lets the operation change the record's *other* fields only while the record is in Draft. 5.13 could say when the status may move, not when the rest of the row may change.

**4. Every write is held to the model (§6).** However a Contract is performed — coded, interpreted, or by a runtime engine — every Record mutation must satisfy `### Schema`, `### Immutable`, `owner`/`!owner` and every State governing the Record before it commits. When a realization may perform a Contract without interpretation is realization, not language.

## Editorial decisions

The 5.13 text contradicted itself in places. 6.0 chose explicitly:

- Current performer declarations win over §18's obsolete suggestion that a step's first words name its performer.
- The universal immediate-Summary rule (with its Persona exception) wins over examples that omitted Summary.
- All five optional Style sub-blocks are optional; the old count of four was stale.
- File kinds follow the current facet definitions; Style stays inline-only.
- Directory naming follows the canonical layout example: a full root namespace, then one directory per added segment.
- A qualified reference selects its starting namespace and then follows the one resolution algorithm.
- A required alias resolves through a mapping or a Capability binding.
- `triggered-by` lists promoted process intents among its sources and Flows among its targets, as the declared endpoint sets already allowed.
- A decision outcome may `reads` as well as `emits`, `invokes` and `mutates`.
- A UI piece with a capability of its own may become a child intent; a surface serving the same intent may stay a View beside its siblings.

## Still open

Three type questions 5.13 left unreconciled are recorded, not resolved by widening the closed taxonomy: a Record-valued `list(...)` derives `refs` to a Record while the `refs` row names field to field; a promoted record-intent is read and mutated as an intent while `reads`/`mutates` name Records; a promoted process's top-level Steps keep Flow grammar while the taxonomy admits no intent-origin `reads`, `mutates` or `emits`. Each needs its own decision.

## Implemented

Amy implements 6.0 (2026-10-26): the graph reads `plays`, grants and guards and reports their errors; reading and rewriting a model keeps every element above (her own repository's 25 intent files come back with the same graph); the runtime refuses an illegal move, a guard's state, a deletion no State allows, a changed immutable field and self-action under `!owner`, and widens scope only by a grant.
