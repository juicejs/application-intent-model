# Proposal (AIM 6.5) — Output: a thing the work produces

Status: **written into the specification (AIM 6.5), 2026-11-13, pending the owner's review of the text** · Author: Michael & Claude working session · Affects: §2 (`nature`), §3 (`file`, schema fields), §6.0 (facet count, promoted forms), a new §6.16 Output, §6.2 (Operation), §6.3 (Process), §6.4 (Persona), §6.8 (Capability), §6.9 (Style), §6.11 (Knowledge), §6.12–§6.15 (the actors), §7 (two new verbs, `produces` and `needs`, five widened rows), §10 (diagnostics), §12 (no migration)

## Where this comes from

Four real cases, all from 2026-11.

1. **A prospective customer, 2026-11-13.** He compared Amy with an assistant's skills: there he says one sentence, gets a result, corrects it, and the correction sticks. His example is a skill that writes quick recipes, which he "trained" to leave out gluten and nuts because of allergies. In Amy he had to explain everything before anything existed. He also wants to hand his existing skills over and have them built.
2. **The owner's own session the same day** (workspace Restaurant Micko 2). One sentence: "ich muss email texte erstellen für einladungen, mit der entsprechend form und formulierung". The Architect asked seven questions over three rounds and wrote a valid 6.4 model: a `Record: Einladung`, a `Record: Einladungstext`, an `Operation: EinladungstextErstellen` whose only Input was the id of a stored Einladung, and a Persona. The Architect said twice that there was no sequence of steps. Released and used, the work refused the first request: an invitation had to be entered first, and nothing in the model creates one. No text was ever produced.
3. **"Speisekarte als PDF erzeugen"**, same workspace. The owner supplied the look and arrangement of the menu. The look went into a Style. The arrangement went into a requirement that the Architect reported as attached to nothing: "Die Regel „LAYOUT" ist noch keinem Schritt zugeordnet." A View has `### Layout` for this (§6.5); a document the work hands over has no place for it.
4. **Exam correction.** The owner grades written exams today with an assistant skill: scanned handwritten answer sheets go in, a formatted grading report comes out, and the skill carries the solutions and the rubric. He wants to supply the solutions once, and then, wherever the work runs, upload one student's exam and receive its correction.

5. **A building company waiting for concrete.** The owner's test of the draft, 2026-11-13: a model of an organisation, not of software. The concrete is made by a supplier, has properties it must meet, and a crew cannot pour a foundation without it. Nothing in it is information a tool makes. If the facet only fits generated text, an organisation's model has no place for what it makes and what it waits for.

A sixth case is already in the specification. §6.8 names "generate a DDEX file" and "render a PDF" as Capabilities. That names the function. Nothing names the DDEX file or the document.

Two outside readings support the direction. Anthropic's "Building effective agents" recommends the simplest implementation that works, often a single model call with retrieval, and separates predefined workflows from agents that choose their own steps. A note by Wolfgang Rams (2026-11-13) asks for a clear contract at every model step — defined input, defined output format, defined purpose — with a check after it. Both say the owner should be able to specify a useful result without first designing how it is made.

## The problem

In all five cases the thing the owner cares about is a noun: his recipes, the invitation text, the menu, the correction, the concrete. He defines it by what one contains or is, what it is made from, the form it comes in and what must hold for every one of them.

What 6.4 can already say, to be exact about the gap:

- **A transient structured value.** A Record used only as a part has no rows, no identity and no persistence, and its field rules and `### Constraints` still hold (§3, Parts). An Operation can return `part(<Record>)`. So 6.4 can describe a recipe as an Operation that takes a wish and returns a part. Case 2 failed because of the model the Architect wrote, not because 6.4 could not express the text.

What 6.4 cannot say:

- **The deliverable as a thing with its own contract.** In 6.4 the contract is split: the shape in a Record, the guarantees in each Operation that returns it, the arrangement in a requirement, the look in a Style. Nothing is addressable as "the correction". Two operations and a process step that hand over the same kind of thing each restate its guarantees.
- **Its form.** Nothing says that the thing is handed over as text, as a document or as a file in a named standard. `file` (§3) is a document value with no shape and no rules.
- **Its arrangement.** `### Layout` exists for a View only (case 3).
- **Asking for one.** A Persona can perform an Operation or reach a View. It cannot be said to ask for a deliverable. So the owner and the Architect must think in an act, and tools show an action somebody performs. The owner rejected that as the item: it "doesn't describe our output item".
- **Needing one.** Work can say which Records it reads and which Events it waits for. It cannot say that it needs what other work or another party produces. In case 5 the link between the delivery and the pour is only in the words of a step.
- **A result that is not information.** Concrete, a poured foundation, a repaired roof. A Record can hold data about one. Nothing says that the thing itself is what the work produces.

Cases 1 and 2 are evidence of the last point: with only acts and stored data to reach for, a valid model came out that could not produce its one result.

## Proposal

A sixteenth facet, **Output**: the contract of a result of work. A View is what people look at and use. An Output is what the work hands over. The word is the one process description already uses: the O of SIPOC, and the inputs and outputs of a process in ISO 9001. One party's Output is the next one's input.

### 6.16 Output

**Purpose.** An Output is a result of work that is handed over — a recipe, an invitation text, a corrected exam, a menu as a document, a DDEX file, ready-mixed concrete on site, a poured foundation — named by what it is, never by who or what makes it. It may be information or a thing in the world. It commits to what one is made from, what one contains or is, the form it is handed over in and what holds for every one. It says nothing about how one is made. It never acts and it is never kept: work that keeps one, or data about one, stores it in a Record.

**Form.**

| Sub-block | Required | Holds |
|---|---|---|
| `### Summary` | yes | What the thing is and what it is for. |
| `### Input` | no | What one is made from, in a `schema` fence, with the grammar of an Operation's Input (§6.2). A field may be a `file`. |
| `### Contains` | no | The content of one, or for a thing in the world the properties it is described by, in a `schema` fence, with the grammar of §3. Absent for free text. |
| `### Constraints` | no | Rules on the content, one bullet each, as on a Record (§6.1). |
| `### Format` | no | The form one is handed over in, one bullet. The first word is `text`, `data`, `document`, `file` or `physical`; a named standard, format or carrier may follow in words after ` — `. Absent means the form is not stated; a realization that makes the Output itself hands over text. |
| `### Ensures` | no | What holds for every one, one bullet each, with its edges. |
| `### Layout` | no | Arrangements the owner stated for a `document`, one bullet each, in their words, under the rules of a View's Layout (§6.5). |

**Example.**

````markdown
## Output: Rezept

### Summary

Ein schnelles Rezept für eine Mahlzeit.

### Input

```schema
wunsch: string required
personen: integer
```

### Contains

```schema
titel: string required
zutaten: list(string) required
schritte: list(string) required
minuten: integer required max(30)
```

### Ensures

- Enthält kein Gluten und keine Nüsse — [satisfies](aim:kueche#Requirements[ALLERGIEN])
````

**Edges.** Declares `reads` (stored Records it is made from), `consults` (Knowledge), `invokes` to a Capability only, and `satisfies`, inside Ensures bullets. Its Input and Contains derive `holds` from each `part(...)`. Receives `produces` from Operations, Processes and intents with Steps, and from a Persona, an Organisation, a Software, a Service or an intent with a Role; and `needs` from Operations, Processes and intents with Steps. `produces` derives `produced-by`, `needs` derives `needed-by`.

**Rules.**

- **It never acts.** An Output declares no `mutates`, `emits`, `exposes`, `navigates`, `subscribes` or `triggers`, and it invokes no Operation and no Process. Work that changes something while making one is an Operation that `produces` it.
- **It is never kept.** An Output has no rows, no identity and no `ref(...)`. Work that keeps one is an Operation that `produces` it and `mutates` a Record whose fields take its values; a `document` or `file` is kept in a `file` field.
- **The accountable producer.** A `produces` edge names who answers for the Output, never who or what carries out the making. From an Operation, a Process or a step, the Output is made as part of that work, for whoever performs it. From a Persona, an Organisation, a Software, a Service or an intent with a Role, that actor is its accountable producer: it makes one or has one made. Where a realization makes it, the making runs on the actor's behalf and under the actor's identity. Every `reads` of the Output is that actor's read, narrowed by ownership as any read of theirs is (§3). An Operation that `produces` an Output hands it back; its `### Returns` need not restate the Output's Contains.
- **Who or what makes it is open.** An Output may be made by a person, an organisation or an outside system, or realized by code, by a single model call, by a workflow or by an agent. The model states its commitments and the dependencies it requires; how it is made is chosen by whoever makes it, except where intent prescribes steps.
- **Needed by work.** Work that cannot be done without an Output `needs` it: the Output is that work's input. Within one case, a step that needs an Output an earlier step produced gets that one. An Output that work needs and nothing in the model produces is supplied from outside and is owed, as a Record that is read and never mutated owes its contents (§8). `needs` states a dependency, not a wait: work that waits for an Output to arrive waits on the Event that announces it (§6.3).
- **When steps are intent.** Drafting, checking and revising inside the making are realization. A step belongs in the model when its occurrence, its order or its hand-over is a commitment — a teacher approves a correction before it is released, a grade is recorded. That work is a Process or an Operation that `produces` the Output (§6.3).
- **The model stays the authority.** Everything that shapes an Output is in the model: its Contains, Constraints, Format, Layout and Ensures, the Requirements it satisfies and the Knowledge it consults. A realization may use instructions, templates and examples to implement those commitments; it MUST NOT carry a rule for the Output that the model does not state. A correction meant for one Output changes that one. A correction meant for every later one is a change to the model.
- **Acceptance.** Where no realization makes or carries the Output — a person, an organisation or an outside system makes it, or it is `physical` — its Contains, Constraints and Ensures are what one is accepted against. Accepting is work: an Operation or a step that `needs` the Output states it. A realization records the acceptance and never claims a rule as held that nobody checked.
- **Content and representation.** Contains and Constraints describe the content; Format describes the representation that is handed over. A realization that makes or carries an Output MUST check the content — required fields, types, value rules and Constraints — before it renders or hands over anything, whatever the Format. `data` hands the content over as its fields. `text`, `document` and `file` render it: the representation MUST carry the whole content and state nothing the content does not hold. Where the Format names a standard that can be validated, the representation MUST be valid in it. An Output without Contains is free text, and only its Format and Ensures apply.
- **Judged guarantees.** Ensures prose is judged, as an Operation's is (§6.2): a realization records the judgment and never claims a judged rule as proven. A rule that can be stated on the content belongs in Contains or Constraints, where it is checked.
- **Dependencies.** A function the Output must get from outside — a certified signature, a rating from a scoring service, validation by the body that owns a standard — is a Capability it `invokes`, and the Ensures bullet says what the function is used for. Reading an Input and rendering a Format are the realization's responsibility and need no declared Capability; a model declares one for reading or rendering only when the work depends on a particular function doing it.
- **Input by document.** A `file` field in Input is a document the requester supplies. The Output owns the commitments made from it.
- **The look.** A `document` takes the Style in reach, as a View does (§6.9). `### Layout` states only what the owner specified and declares no edges; pixels and templates are realization.
- **A facet until earned.** An Output stays a facet on the level that shares it. One with a purpose, requirements and knowledge of its own may become an intent (§11): `nature: output`, its Summary and requirements at the top, and its blocks as top-level sections of the same names. A promoted Output states its Format even when it is `text`: an intent with a top-level `## Format` is an Output endpoint.

**Diagnostics.** Hard error: `produces` or `needs` to anything other than an Output, or from a source the taxonomy does not allow; an edge an Output may not declare (above); `invokes` from an Output to anything other than a Capability; a Format whose first word is not `text`, `data`, `document`, `file` or `physical`; `### Layout` on an Output whose Format is not `document`; `### Constraints` without `### Contains`; a `ref(...)` to an Output. Informational: an Output nothing produces and nothing needs (orphan); an Output that work needs and nothing produces, which is owed from outside; an Output with no Constraints and no Ensures, which says what one is but not what a good one is; a Format naming a standard while the Output invokes no Capability and none is in reach, where the standard's function is owed; an Operation whose Returns restates the Contains of an Output it does not produce. Reviewer guidance, not a diagnostic: an Operation that only returns a part and changes nothing reads as the making of an Output; the reviewer asks the owner.

**Since.** 6.5.

### The new verbs

| Verb | From | To | Meaning | Kind |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `produces` | operation, process, intent with Steps, persona, organisation, software, service, intent with Role | output | Answers for one Output: from work, it is made as part of that work; from an actor, the actor makes one or has one made, under that actor's identity. | declared |
| `needs` | operation, process, intent with Steps | output | Cannot be done without one Output: the Output is this work's input. | declared |
| `produced-by` | output | operation / process / persona / organisation / software / service / intent | Inverse of `produces`. | derived |
| `needed-by` | output | operation / process / intent | Inverse of `needs`. | derived |

Five existing rows widen by one endpoint. Nothing else in the taxonomy changes.

| Verb | Change |
|---|---|
| `reads` | From: + output |
| `consults` | From: + output |
| `invokes` | From: + output, to a capability only |
| `satisfies` | From: + output; `satisfied-by` To: + output |
| `holds` | From: + the Input or Contains of an output |

**Schema fields (§3).** An Output's Input and Contains are data blocks like an Operation's Input and Returns: their fields are addressed and identified the same way, and a part in them is checked when the Output is accepted or handed over and carries no lasting obligation.

**Traceability (§5).** An Output is inside traceability. It is reached by `produces` and `needs`, can be an orphan, and its `satisfies` edges count toward a requirement's `satisfied-by`.

### What changes elsewhere

- **§2.** `nature` gains `output`.
- **§3.** The `file` paragraph reads "An Operation or an Output…"; the Parts paragraph names an Output's Input and Contains among the holders.
- **§6.0.** Sixteen facets. Output joins the facets with a defined promoted form.
- **§6.2 Operation, §6.3 Process.** May declare `produces` and `needs` in an Ensures or Expects bullet or a step.
- **§6.4 Persona, §6.12–§6.15 the actors.** `### Access` may declare `produces`: the actor makes one or has one made, and answers for it.
- **§6.8 Capability.** Receives `invokes` from an Output.
- **§6.9 Style.** Applies to a `document` Output as to a View.
- **§6.11 Knowledge.** Receives `consults` from an Output.
- **§10.** The diagnostics above.

### Existing models stay valid

6.5 adds a heading type, two verbs, a `nature` value and one endpoint on five rows. It changes no existing meaning and narrows no allowed realization. A model that describes a produced thing as an Operation returning a part stays valid and is never rewritten by migration: whether it is really an Output is the Architect's judgement, made with the owner (§12).

## The cases, written with it

### Recipes

The example in the facet block above, with the level that shares it:

```markdown
## Requirements

- **ALLERGIEN** — Kein Rezept enthält Gluten oder Nüsse. — from: the owner, 2026-11-13

## Persona: Koch

### Access

- Lässt sich ein Rezept schreiben — [produces](aim:kueche#Output:Rezept)
```

"Training" is the requirement line. The customer adds "no nuts" once; it is a requirement with its source, every recipe satisfies it, and it is judged on every recipe. "Thirty minutes at most" is a rule on the content, so it sits on the field and is checked. A single model call is a sufficient realization.

### Invitation e-mail text

Case 2 again. The occasion and the date are Input, so the owner's sentence can feed it. Nothing is stored, so nothing has to exist first.

````markdown
## Output: Einladungstext

### Summary

Der Text einer Einladungs-E-Mail, mit Betreff, in der Form, die zum Anlass passt.

### Input

```schema
anlass: string required enum(Hochzeit, Geburtstag, Firmenfeier)
termin: date
ort: string
empfaenger: string
```

### Contains

```schema
betreff: string required
text: string required
```

### Ensures

- Hochzeit und Firmenfeier sind förmlich mit Sie angeschrieben, Geburtstag locker mit du — [satisfies](aim:root#Requirements[ANREDE-NACH-ANLASS])
- Betreff und Text passen in Form und Formulierung zum Anlass — [satisfies](aim:root#Requirements[FORM-PASST-ZUM-ANLASS])
````

6.4 could have said this with one Operation returning a part. The model that was actually written had two Records, two Operations and a Persona, and could not run. With an Output the owner's own noun is the node, and the Persona asks for it.

### Exam correction

First the deliverable alone. The solutions are Knowledge, supplied once. The student's exam is a `file` in the Input. Reading it is the realization's responsibility, so the model declares no Capability for it. A school that must have a particular reading service do it would declare that function as a Capability the Output invokes.

````markdown
## Requirements

- **NACH-MUSTERLOESUNG** — Jede Aufgabe wird nach der Musterlösung und ihrem Punkteschema bewertet.
- **BEGRUENDET** — Jeder Punktabzug ist begründet.

## Knowledge: Musterloesung

### Label

Musterlösung

### Summary

Die Lösungen der Klausur mit dem Punkteschema je Aufgabe. Die Grundlage jeder Bewertung.

## Record: Aufgabenbewertung

### Summary

Die Bewertung einer Aufgabe. Nur als Teil einer Korrektur verwendet.

### Schema

```schema
aufgabe: string required
punkte: decimal required min(0)
maximal: decimal required
begruendung: string
```

### Constraints

- Die Punkte einer Aufgabe übersteigen ihr Maximum nicht.

## Output: Korrektur

### Summary

Die Korrektur der Klausur einer Studentin oder eines Studenten.

### Input

```schema
klausur: file required
```

### Contains

```schema
name: string required
aufgaben: list(part(kurs.klausur#Aufgabenbewertung)) required
gesamtpunkte: decimal required
note: string
```

### Constraints

- Die Gesamtpunktzahl ist die Summe der Punkte aller Aufgaben.

### Format

- document — ein Bericht je Klausur

### Ensures

- Die Antworten stammen aus der hochgeladenen Klausur und aus nichts sonst.
- Jede Aufgabe ist nach der Musterlösung bewertet — [consults](aim:kurs.klausur#Knowledge:Musterloesung), [satisfies](aim:kurs.klausur#Requirements[NACH-MUSTERLOESUNG])
- Jeder Punktabzug nennt seinen Grund — [satisfies](aim:kurs.klausur#Requirements[BEGRUENDET])

## Persona: Dozent

### Access

- Lädt eine Klausur hoch und erhält ihre Korrektur — [produces](aim:kurs.klausur#Output:Korrektur)
````

This is sufficient intent for what the owner asked: he supplies the solutions once, uploads one student's exam and receives the correction. How it is made is the realization's choice. Reading, grading each task, adding up and rendering may be a chain of steps with checks between them; the model does not prescribe them. It does commit to what the chain must deliver: the sum and the maximum are Constraints and are checked, the grading is judged against the solutions, and the document carries exactly the checked content.

When the Dozent must approve a correction before it counts, that approval is a commitment, and it is a step. The Output does not change; a Process produces it:

```markdown
## Process: KlausurKorrigieren

### Summary

Von der abgegebenen Klausur zur freigegebenen Note.

### Steps

1. Die Korrektur wird erstellt — [produces](aim:kurs.klausur#Output:Korrektur)
2. Der Dozent prüft die Korrektur und gibt sie frei — [needs](aim:kurs.klausur#Output:Korrektur), [invokes](aim:kurs.klausur#Operation:KorrekturFreigeben)
```

`Operation: KorrekturFreigeben` names the Dozent in its Authz and records the grade in a Record; both are left out here.

### Access

### Summary

## Process: FundamentGiessen

### Summary

Vom bestellten Beton zum gegossenen Fundament.

### Steps

## The admission test (§6.0)

1. **A commitment no existing facet and property can carry without losing checkability.** Five commitments have no carrier in 6.4: the form a result is handed over in and its validity in a named standard; the stated arrangement of a document that is not a View; that an actor makes a result or has it made and answers for it; that work depends on a result other work or another party produces; and that the result is a thing in the world with rules it is accepted against. A sixth is carried today only by repetition: the guarantees of one deliverable, restated in every Operation that returns it, with nothing that keeps them the same. The shape of the content is not among them: a part carries it already.
2. **Demonstrated need in real models.** The five cases above. Case 2 is a valid 6.4 model that could not produce its one result; it shows what Architects and owners write when the deliverable has no node. Case 5 is the owner's own test and is not yet a model that was run.
3. **Complete.** The block with every part; the node type `output`; the verbs `produces` and `needs` with their endpoints and derived inverses; the five widened rows and the schema-field rule; its place in traceability and that it can be an orphan; its diagnostics; the statement that existing models stay valid.

## Decisions to confirm

The draft takes a position on each. All are the owner's.

1. **The name.** `Output` is the owner's own word, mirrors `Input`, and is the established word of process description (SIPOC, ISO 9001) for goods, services, documents and data alike. `Deliverable` is narrower: something owed to a recipient under a contract or a project; a poured foundation is an Output of the crew's work before anybody calls it a deliverable. `Artifact` and `Document` fit information only. `Product` and `Result` are avoided: a shop's product is a Record, and "result" already means what an Operation returns.
2. **Any result of work** (owner, 2026-11-13: "that works"), information or a thing in the world, with `physical` as the fifth form and acceptance where no tool can check.
3. **`needs`** as the verb by which work says which Output it depends on. `uses` is the alternative; `requires` and `takes` already mean something else in the specification.
4. **Asked for directly, with an accountable producer.** A Persona may `produce` an Output with no Operation in between; the making runs under that Persona's identity. The alternative is that an Operation always stands between the two.
5. **One facet for every form**, with content (Contains, Constraints) and representation (Format) kept apart.
6. **`### Constraints` on an Output**, as on a Record.
7. **`### Layout` on a document Output**, under a View's rules. Case 3 is the evidence. It can be left out of 6.5 and added later.
8. **Who or what makes it is open**: a person, an organisation, code, a model call, a workflow or an agent; steps are intent only when they are commitments.
9. **The promoted form**, marked by a top-level `## Format`.

## Not in this proposal

- **A restriction on what may answer a Capability.** The first draft added "never answered by instructions alone" to §6.8. It is taken out: it narrows allowed realizations, so it needs its own proposal with its own compatibility argument. What this proposal needs is already in the authority rule: no rule for the work may live only in a realization. A tool remains free to offer only code and services as answers.
- **Marking where a field's value comes from.** A name, a date or an amount in an Output should come from the Input, a Record, Knowledge or a Capability, and never be supplied from nowhere. Whether a field can declare that is open.
- A `cross-owner(...)` grant on a `produces` edge.
- Keeping a history of produced Outputs. Work that needs one keeps a Record.
- `part(<Output>)` in a Record's schema, to hold an Output's shape without restating its fields.
- How a tool lets an owner try an Output and correct its rules. That is a tool's surface, not language.

## Changes after review (2026-11-13)

- `produces` now names the accountable producer, with the identity that governs reads.
- Content and representation are separated, and what is checked is stated for each.
- `holds` is extended to an Output's Input and Contains; five rows widen, not four.
- The missing-renderer diagnostic is removed. Reading and rendering are realization unless the work depends on a declared function.
- The Capability restriction is taken out of this proposal.
- The problem statement no longer claims that a Record commits to rows. The admission argument rests on the deliverable contract, Format and Layout, and direct request.
- Added: `### Constraints`, the realization rule, when steps are intent, and the two effects of a correction.

## Widened (owner, 2026-11-13)

The owner tested the draft against an organisation: a building company waiting for concrete. Three changes follow.

- An Output is any result of work, information or a thing in the world. `physical` joins the forms.
- Where no tool makes or carries an Output, its rules are what it is accepted against, and accepting is a step.
- A second verb, `needs`, lets work say which Output it depends on. A wait stays a wait on an Event.

Open after the widening: whether an Event can say that it announces an Output, so that the wait in step 2 of the concrete case and the Output in steps 3 and 4 are linked by more than words.
