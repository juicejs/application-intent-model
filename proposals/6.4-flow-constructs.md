# Proposal (AIM 6.4) — the process, filled in: races, an interrupting deadline, steps over a collection, timed waits, criteria as rules

Status: **agreed and written into the specification (AIM 6.4), 2026-11-08** — drafted 2026-11-07, revised twice after a second model's reviews of the execution semantics (2026-11-07, 2026-11-08) · Author: Michael & Claude working session · Affects: §6.2 (Operation — Contract as renamed by 6.3 Part 5: `### Criteria`), §6.3 (Process — Flow as renamed by 6.3 Part 4; what a case is; Steps: what a wait hears, waits on several Events, `for each`, `after:` / `until`; `Ends on`), §6.7 (Trigger: `### Ends`), §10 (diagnostics), the Paths and BPMN projections

## Where this comes from

The NASA example (`examples/nasa`, written against 6.3) holds three real processes: lifecycle gates with a rework loop, a launch countdown with a hold and a window, anomaly resolution entered by an Event. Set beside BPMN 2 and DMN, each of them had a place where the language ran out and the commitment was left in prose or in the realization:

- the window closes while the countdown is **waiting** for the lander: the deadline scrubs the record, but nothing ends the run still waiting at that step;
- lander ready or scrub called, whichever comes first, is a **race**, and §6.3 says joint work never expresses one;
- the board holds a review no earlier than ten business days after it was opened: a **wait on time** inside the steps, which today can only be a Trigger that starts other work;
- close **every** action, poll **every** station: a step over a collection, written as one Operation whose criteria say "every";
- the launch commit criteria for weather are a **table of rules** in real life and a sentence under an outcome in the model.

The owner: do not adopt BPMN; fill the gaps the AIM way, so more real-world cases can be said. A second model reviewed the first draft and found five places where two realizations could both follow the text and behave differently; this revision fixes each, and two of its forks were decided by the owner (see Decided).

## The AIM way

- **No new verb.** The closed taxonomy (§7) stays. Every fill is a property of a construct that exists: a word on a step, a sub-block on a Trigger or a Operation.
- **Declared where it acts.** On the waiting step, on the deadline, on the deciding operation; derived structure follows.
- **Prose binds; the structured form is checkable, and where it is present it is the whole commitment.** A rule written as a line is not a sketch of the words beside it; it is the decision.
- **One behaviour.** Every fill says what happens at the edges — an Event that came early, two that come at once, a field with no value, an item that fails — so that two realizations cannot both be right and differ.
- **Per case, in the case's clock.** Everything counts in the `timezone` and `calendar` in reach and belongs to the case as §6.6 and §6.7 define it.
- **Existing models stay valid, unchanged.** Every fill is additive; a file that uses none reads as it did.

## Part 0 — what a case is, and what a wait hears (rules 6.2 left unsaid)

§6.6 and §6.7 speak of "the case" and never say what one is; §6.3 says a waiting step "continues with the next step when the Event arrives for this case" and does not say whether an Event that arrived before the step was reached counts. The NASA countdown shows both gaps: SpaceX may report the lander ready while the weather is still being polled, and a step over the stations runs several things at once. Every fill below rests on these rules.

**A case is one run of one process** — a Process facet or a promoted process — started by an entry: an actor invoking it, a Trigger, a subscription at its top, or a step of another case invoking it.

- **A child case.** A step that invokes a process starts a child case; the step completes when the child case ends — that is the join. A run started by an emitted Event (a subscriber with steps, §6.3) is a separate case, not a child.
- **An item run.** A step over a collection (Part 3) runs one item run per item. An item run's record is the item — the row, or the list element; it is part of its case, not a case of its own.
- **Belonging.** An Event belongs to the case that emitted it and to every case whose record its Payload references (§6.6). An Event referencing an item's row belongs to that item run. A child case's Events are the child's, unless they reference the parent's record.
- **Hearing (decided, owner 2026-11-07).** A wait hears every Event of its case since the case began. A case keeps every Event that belongs to it in one order; the realization keeps that order and it is the only order that "first" means. A wait reached after its Event arrived finds it and continues at once. Two Events that arrive together are ordered by the case's order, never undefined.
- **Taking.** A wait takes the earliest Event of its kind that belongs to it and that no other wait of the same case has taken; two successive waits on the same Event wait for two occurrences. An item run's wait takes Events of its item; two waits with nothing to tell them apart compete, in the order they were reached.
- **Ending across boundaries (decided, owner 2026-11-08).** When a case ends — by its last step, by an Event under `Ends on` (Part 2), or by a deadline's `### Ends` (Part 2) — the child cases its steps started end with it, at once, and their own cleanup does not run; item runs end with their step; the parent's join never completes. A separate case started by an Event is unaffected.

## Part 1 — a wait on several Events is a race; `continues:` says which go on

**§6.3 Steps, Waiting.** A step with several `subscribes` waits for whichever of those Events comes first in the case's order (Part 0). The first ends the wait; the others are not taken by this step. After the edges, `— continues: <Event>, <Event>` names the Events that go on to the next step; an Event not named ends the case at this step — its own subscribers elsewhere still run, as for any Event. Without `continues:`, every one of them goes on: the step waited for any of them. Events are named by facet name, as outcomes are.

A race is for **genuine alternatives**: two things that may happen, of which the case wants the first — an offer accepted or declined, a part delivered or back-ordered. An Event that must end the case whenever it comes is not an alternative; it is written once, under `Ends on` (Part 2), never as a side of a race. The reason is Part 0: a race takes the earliest Event in the case's order, so a readiness that came early would win over a scrub that came later, and the case would go on into a scrubbed countdown.

```markdown
4. Wait for the customer's answer — [subscribes](aim:juice.sales#Event:OfferAccepted), [subscribes](aim:juice.sales#Event:OfferDeclined) — continues: OfferAccepted
```

(A sales process, not NASA: the countdown's wait for the lander has no genuine alternative, which is itself a finding — most "whichever comes first" in real processes are a wait and a cancellation.)

**Diagnostics.** Hard error: a `continues:` naming an Event the step does not subscribe to. Informational: a single-subscription step with `continues:` (nothing to choose); a race whose Events can never belong to one case (none is emitted within the case or references its record, §6.6); an Event subscribed at a step and also listed under `Ends on` (the step's side is dead: the case ends first).

**Projection.** An event-based gateway with one catching event per subscription; the Events not continued lead to an end event.

## Part 2 — what ends a case: an Event under `Ends on`, a deadline with `### Ends`

### An Event that ends the case

**§6.3 Process, new sub-block `### Ends on`; on a promoted process, top-level `## Ends on`.** Bullets of Event addresses in the bracket form a Trigger's Schedule uses: `- [<intent>#Event:<Name>]`. When such an Event belonging to the case is placed in the case's order, the case ends at that point, at once: nothing after it in the order advances the case, no step starts, no wait completes, and its child cases and item runs end with it (Part 0). There is no cleanup: the Event is already the record of what happened — a scrub was done by `Scrub` before it emitted `LaunchScrubbed`. Anything that must happen on ending is the emitter's work.

```markdown
## Ends on

- [nasa.esdmd.artemis3.countdown#Event:LaunchScrubbed]
```

With this the countdown's wait for the lander subscribes to `LanderReady` alone, and a scrub — called by the launch director at any step, or by the weather, the range, the go poll or the window — ends the case wherever it is, before or after the readiness came.

**Diagnostics.** Hard error: an `Ends on` address that is not an Event. Informational: an `Ends on` Event that can never belong to the case (never emitted within it and never referencing its record); an `Ends on` Event also subscribed at a step (the step's side is dead).

**Projection.** An interrupting boundary message event on the whole process, leading to an end event.

### A deadline that ends the case

**§6.7 Trigger, new sub-block `### Ends`.** On a Trigger of kind `deadline`, one bullet: `the case`. When the deadline expires for a case:

1. **The case is interrupted at once.** No Event advances it, no step starts, no wait completes; nothing of the case runs but what the deadline triggers.
2. **The triggered work runs**, as the cleanup: it records what must be recorded and moves the case's record where a State requires (the scrub marks the countdown scrubbed).
3. **When the triggered work completes, the case ends.** It hears no further Events.
4. **If the triggered work does not complete**, the case stays interrupted and a realization MUST show it as such — like owed work with no performer, a question for the owner, never silent. Recovery is explicit: the realization offers to run the triggered work again or to end the case by hand; neither happens on its own. A case ended by hand with its triggered work incomplete carries a **run-time finding, *cleanup incomplete***, kept with the case and shown with it; it is a fact of that run, not a diagnostic of the graph, and no static check reports it.
5. **Child cases and item runs end with it** (Part 0): the ones its steps started, at once, their own cleanup not run; the parent's join never completes.

A deadline without `### Ends` behaves as today: it starts work and interrupts nothing.

```markdown
## Trigger: WindowCloses

### Summary

Three hours after the call to stations the window has closed; the count is interrupted, scrubbed, and ends.

### Kind

- deadline

### Schedule

- 3 hours after [nasa.esdmd.artemis3.countdown#Event:CountdownStarted] — unless [nasa.esdmd.artemis3.countdown#Event:Liftoff]

### Fires

- [triggers](aim:nasa.esdmd.artemis3.countdown#Operation:Scrub)

### Ends

- the case
```

The review-closure deadline keeps no `### Ends`: it escalates and the review goes on. The anomaly deadline keeps none either: escalation, not abandonment.

**Ending a step is not in this version.** A first draft offered `the step` — abandon the current step and go on. A deadline anchored on a case's start cannot tell which step it governs: the anomaly timer starts at detection and would abandon triage, proposal or the director's decision, whichever happened to be current; a process with phases has several active steps. Step interruption arrives when a deadline can name the step activation it bounds — a wait that carries its own limit — and not before.

**Rules.** `### Ends` is allowed only on a deadline (per case); a schedule has no case to end. `unless` disarms it as any deadline's.

**Diagnostics.** Hard error: `### Ends` on a Trigger whose kind is not `deadline`; a bullet other than `the case`. Informational: `### Ends` with no `unless` Event (it will always fire; ask whether that is meant).

**Projection.** An interrupting boundary timer on the whole process, leading through the triggered work to a terminate end event.

## Part 3 — a step over a collection

**§6.3 Steps, new words.**

```text
<n>. <words> — for each [<address>] [as <input>] [in order | in order of [<field address>]] — <edges>
```

- **The collection** is either a list field of the case's record — `<intent>#Record:<Name>.<field>` whose type is `list(...)` — or a Record whose rows belong to the case — `<intent>#Record:<Name>` with a `ref(...)` field to the case's record; where that Record references the case's record through more than one field, the one meant is named: `[<intent>#Record:<Name> by <field>]`.
- **Selected once.** The items are the collection's content when the step starts; items added or removed afterwards do not join or leave the step.
- **Order.** A list keeps its order. Rows have no order of their own: a step over a Record runs its items jointly unless `in order of [<field address>]` names a field of that Record to order by; `in order` alone is allowed for a list and is a hard error for a Record.
- **Joint or sequential.** Items are joint work and MAY run concurrently; `in order` or `in order of` runs them one after another. The next step is the join: it begins when every item has completed. An empty collection completes the step at once.
- **The item reaches the operation as its Input.** The invoked operation declares an Input field that takes the item: a scalar of the list's item type, a `ref(...)` to the Record's row, or a `part(...)` of its shape. Where more than one Input field could take it, `as <input>` names the receiving field; with exactly one, it may be left out.
- **A failed item fails the step.** Completion means every item completed; an item whose operation did not complete leaves the step incomplete, and the case goes to `## On Error` for that step as for any failed step. Completed is not succeeded: a deciding operation's outcomes apply per item and carry their own consequences per item, and never branch the case — `continues:` is not written on a step over a collection.
- **The gate after the collection is a step.** What every item's outcome amounts to is decided by the next step: an operation that reads what the items recorded and decides. Polling every station is a step over the stations; whether the count goes on is the go poll after it.

```markdown
7. Poll every station — for each [nasa.esdmd.artemis3.countdown#Record:Countdown.stations] as station — [invokes](aim:nasa.esdmd.artemis3.countdown#Operation:PollStation)
8. Go/No-Go — [invokes](aim:nasa.esdmd.artemis3.countdown#Operation:PollGoNoGo) — continues: Go
```

with `stations: list(string) required` on the countdown, `PollStation` taking `station: string required` and writing the station's answer, and `PollGoNoGo` reading the answers and deciding **Go** (every station answered go) or **NoGo**. Closing every action of a review is the same word over `[nasa.esdmd.artemis3.lifecycle#Record:Action]` with `CloseAction` taking `actionId: string required ref(nasa.esdmd.artemis3.lifecycle#Action.id)`; asking every supplier for a certificate is the same word over the suppliers of an order.

**Diagnostics.** Hard error: an address that is neither a list field of the case's record nor a Record referencing it; a Record referencing it through several fields with no `by`; `in order` on a Record, or `in order of` naming a field not of that Record; `continues:` on a step over a collection; an invoked operation with no Input field that can take the item, or several and no `as`; `as` naming a field that cannot take it. Informational: a step over a collection with no operation (a human step per item is valid and shown per item).

**Projection.** A multi-instance activity, parallel or sequential, over the collection.

## Part 4 — a timed wait

**§6.3 Steps, new words `— after: <n> <unit>` and `— until [<address>]`.** Either delays the step's start.

- `after:` counts from the moment the step is reached, in the units a Trigger's Schedule knows (`minutes`, `hours`, `days`, `business-days`, `weeks`).
- `until` names a `date` or `datetime` field of the case's record. A `datetime` is the instant it holds; a `date` is the start of that day in the `timezone` in reach. A moment already past waits not at all.
- **Resolved once.** The due instant is resolved when the step is reached and kept; a later change to the field does not move an existing wait. Moving it is an explicit act: a new run, or a step that waits again.
- **An absent value waits for one (decided, owner 2026-11-07).** When the step is reached and the field has no value, the case waits at the step until the field is written; that write is the scheduling, and the instant is then resolved from it and kept.
- Both count in the `timezone` and `calendar` in reach. A step that carries one of them and no edges is a wait, not a human step. The wait is bounded like any wait: a deadline Trigger states a limit (§6.7), and `### Ends` may end the case. Durable timing is realization.

```markdown
3. The board conducts the system requirements review — [invokes](aim:nasa.esdmd.artemis3.lifecycle#Operation:ConductGateReview) — after: 10 business-days — continues: Proceed
```

The board has ten working days with the package before it sits. A countdown resuming at a planned moment is `— until [nasa.esdmd.artemis3.countdown#Record:Countdown.resumeAt]`; a cooling-off period, a statutory waiting time, a reminder a week later are the same two words.

**Rules.** `after:` and `until` are written after the edges and before `continues:`. A step may carry one of them, not both.

**Diagnostics.** Hard error: `until` naming a field that is not `date` or `datetime`, or not a field of the case's record; `after:` without a unit the Schedule grammar knows; both words on one step. Informational: a timed wait with no `timezone` in reach, or `business-days` with no `calendar` in reach (ask which).

**Projection.** A timer intermediate event before the activity.

## Part 5 — criteria as rules

**§6.2 Operation and §6.3 Process, new sub-block `### Criteria`.** Allowed only beside `### Decides`. A `criteria` fence, one rule per line:

```text
<Outcome> when <input> <op> <value>[, <input> <op> <value>]...
otherwise <Outcome>
```

- **When present, it is the whole decision.** The rules decide, completely; the outcomes' bullets keep their words, which explain and MUST NOT add a condition the rules do not test. A judgement the rules cannot express is made first, by an earlier human or automated operation, and arrives as an Input the rules read.
- **Inputs are the operation's Input fields.** Nothing else in this version: a read Record names a kind of row, not a row, so `Account.balance` says nothing when the operation reads two accounts.
- **Operators:** `=`, `!=`, `<`, `<=`, `>`, `>=`, `in (a, b, c)`, `between a and b`, `present`, `absent`. Values are literals of the input's type.
- **First hit.** Rules are tried in order and the first that holds decides. `otherwise` is required: no input combination is unmatched.

````markdown
## Operation: PollWeather

### Input

```schema
countdownId: string required ref(nasa.esdmd.artemis3.countdown#Countdown.id)
wind: number required label("Wind at the pad, knots")
lightningWithinTenMiles: boolean required
ceiling: integer required label("Cloud ceiling, feet")
clearingForecastInWindow: boolean required label("The weather officer's forecast: clears inside the window")
```

### Decides

- **Green** — the launch commit criteria for weather are met, or will be inside the window
- **Red** — a criterion is violated and the forecast does not clear it inside the window — [invokes](aim:nasa.esdmd.artemis3.countdown#Operation:Scrub)

### Criteria

```criteria
Red when wind > 30, clearingForecastInWindow = false
Red when lightningWithinTenMiles = true, clearingForecastInWindow = false
Red when ceiling < 1500, clearingForecastInWindow = false
otherwise Green
```
````

The weather officer's forecast is a judgement; it is made before the poll and arrives as an Input, so the rules can read it. This is DMN's idea — inputs, rules, a hit policy — without its language: no expressions beyond a comparison, no functions, nothing a parser cannot check against the schema.

**Diagnostics.** Hard error: a rule naming an outcome not declared under Decides; an input that is not an Input field; a value not of the input's type; no `otherwise`, or two; `### Criteria` without `### Decides`. Informational: a rule no input combination can reach because an earlier rule covers it; an outcome no rule names (declared but undecidable).

**Projection.** A decision table with first-hit policy, one row per rule.

## What is deliberately not filled

- **Ending a step** (Part 2): until a deadline can name the step activation it bounds.
- **Handlers scoped to a case or a phase** (an event subprocess, compensation): they change what a case is, not what a step says. An intent-level `subscribes` starts a run of its own, as today; `### Ends` covers the interrupting case that mattered.
- **Unordered work** (CMMN's case plan): the Steps stay a line; a process that is really a set of things to do in any order is modelled as the Records and operations it holds, with a State that says when it is done.
- **A race between operations** (first-completes-wins between two pieces of work): §6.3 keeps that in prose and procedure verification.
- **Criteria over Record fields or Capability results**: when an input can name a row.

## Acceptance example

Written: `examples/nasa/6.4/`, the countdown in 6.4 form beside its 6.3 form, with eleven scenarios: a lander reported ready before the wait is reached (Part 0); a scrub called during the wait, and a readiness followed by a scrub before the wait is reached (Part 2, `Ends on`); the window expiring while a station's poll is still running, at the same instant as a readiness, and once with a scrub that does not complete (Part 2); the stations list changed after the poll started, one station whose poll fails, and item runs ending with their step (Parts 0 and 3); a resume moment written only after the hold is reached and changed while the case still waits (Part 4); a forecast that clears a violated criterion inside the window (Part 5). No step of the countdown invokes a process, so the child-case rule is stated by the scenarios and not exercised.

## Existing models stay valid

Every 6.3 file is valid 6.4 source, unchanged. The fills are additive words and sub-blocks; a step with one `subscribes`, a deadline without `### Ends`, a Decides without `### Criteria` mean what they meant. The facet is written Process here, as 6.3 Part 4 renames it; Flow reads the same under the old spelling. Part 0 states a behaviour that 6.2 left to the realization; a realization that discarded early Events was within the old text and is outside the new one.

## Decided (owner, 2026-11-07)

- A wait hears every Event of its case since the case began, in the case's one order (Part 0). The alternative — only Events after the wait is reached, with early facts kept as fields — was declined.
- An `until` field with no value makes the case wait for the value; the write schedules it (Part 4). The alternative — the case stops as incomplete — was declined.
- `### Ends` ends the case only; the case is interrupted before the triggered work runs; a cleanup that does not complete leaves the case visibly interrupted with explicit recovery.
- `for each`: selected once at the step's start, list order kept, `in order of` required for a sequential Record, `as` names the receiving Input when ambiguous, a failed item fails the step, the gate after the collection is the next step.
- `### Criteria`, when present, is the whole decision; inputs are Input fields only; `otherwise` is required.
- (2026-11-08) An Event that must end the case whenever it comes is written under `Ends on`, never as a side of a race; the race is for genuine alternatives. The alternative — keep the ending Event in the race and check the record's state at the next step — was declined.
- (2026-11-08) Child cases started by a case's steps end with it, item runs with their step, their own cleanup not run. The alternative — children run to completion — was declined.

## Decided while building it into Amy (owner, 2026-11-08: the open points taken as proposed)

- An Event not continued in a race ends the case there and still runs its own subscribers elsewhere, as any Event.
- A child case's `Ends on` ends the child case only; a parent that must end on the same Event lists it itself. A child that ends by itself completes the parent's step (the join).
- A list of parts may be ordered `in order of [field]`, by a field of the part.
